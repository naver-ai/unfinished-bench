# Cork medical centre — how it works

The Cork medical centre is a GP-led practice on the south side of the city, run by Kellsbrook Health Ireland. It opened originally to serve employer-group members at US companies' Cork sites, and it has been open to the public since. The clinical team is led by Aoife Brennan (GP, Clinical Lead) and includes Wanjiru Kruger (GP), Grace Okafor (Practice Nurse), and Tomás Novak (Lab Liaison). Maeve Daly leads reception, Chiara Quinn is the practice secretary, and Siobhán Kiely manages the practice. The centre also hosts the European trials office for the Kellsbrook Research Institute and part of IT operations, so the building has a mix of clinical, research, and infrastructure staff in and out throughout the working week.

Clinic sessions run each working day, with morning and afternoon slots. We hold a mix of booked appointments and same-day urgent slots, and we keep a small number of appointments open each session for employer-group members who call in with occupational health needs. The public patient list has grown steadily, and the practice now sees a fairly even split of public and private patients. Occupational health work is done under separate contracts with the US employers at their Cork sites; those visits are billed through the employer, not through the Health Plan or the public system.

Blood tests are taken at the centre and sent to the local hospital laboratory. Tomás is the liaison for anything to do with specimen labelling, courier pickups, or results that come back outside the normal turnaround. If a specimen is rejected by the lab, Tomás handles the rerun with the patient and the clinical team. The lab information system we use is the same one the hospital lab uses, not the one in the US, so results arrive through that system and are filed into the patient record by the clinical team.

The centre does not use the US EHR. We run a separate practice system that is configured for Irish GP practice, including the local prescribing and referral workflows. A summary of each patient's care is shared with the US system only where there is a legitimate care relationship — for example, an employer-group member who is seen in Cork and then treated at South Bend. That sharing goes through the interface engine, and the records team in the US handles the filing on their side. If you need something pulled from the US EHR for a patient who is in front of you, ask Maeve or Chiara; they know who to contact in the records office.

A few practical points for anyone new to the building. The practice system is the source of truth for the Cork patient list, appointments, and clinical notes. The US scheduling and registration system is not used here, and you should not try to book a Cork patient into a US clinic through it. If a patient needs a referral to a specialist or for imaging, the clinical team completes the Irish referral forms — do not use the US outgoing referral process. Referral letters go through the local hospital and consultant rooms, not through the US provider portal.

For IT issues, the Cork team works with the infrastructure group in the US day to day, but there is usually someone in the building who can help with the basics first — ask at reception or check the #cork-office channel. The VPN client is the way to reach US systems from Cork, and the IT operations team here keeps an eye on the shared services that run in this office. For anything about the practice system specifically, Siobhán is the first port of call. For the European trials office, the study coordinators sit on the same floor and they handle their own systems (CTMS, EDC, eIRB) through the research institute's processes.

Working with US colleagues takes a little getting used to because of the time difference. Cork is five or six hours ahead of the US depending on the season, so the overlap is short — usually mid-morning our time is early in the US, and late afternoon our time is when the US East Coast is wrapping up. Most day-to-day coordination happens over chat, and the team is used to leaving handovers in writing rather than expecting a live call. If you need something from a US team, send it before mid-afternoon Irish time and you will usually get an answer the same US working day. Anything sent after that waits until the next working day on their side. For urgent matters, the on-call process in the US runs around the clock, but that is only for genuine system outages or patient safety issues — not for routine questions about a referral or a prescription.
