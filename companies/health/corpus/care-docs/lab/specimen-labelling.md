# Specimen labelling and rejection

**Owner:** Laboratory services  
**Last edited by:** Aaliyah Lehmann, Lab Quality Coordinator

## Two patient identifiers

- Use two identifiers on every specimen: full name (as registered) and date of birth or the MRN shown on the wristband.
- The patient's room number, bed, or diagnosis is never an identifier.
- Check identifiers against the wristband and the collection label, not against the patient's spoken name alone.
- For outpatients without a wristband, check against the photo ID or the registration printout from the scheduling and registration system.
- If the patient is unable to identify themselves, confirm identity with the nurse caring for the patient and note who confirmed it.

## Labelling at the bedside

- Label every tube at the bedside, immediately after collection, before moving to the next patient.
- Print the label from the EHR or write it by hand only if the printer is down. Handwritten labels must be legible and include both identifiers, the collector's initials, and the collection time.
- Never pre-label tubes ahead of time, even for a planned draw.
- Never label a tube away from the patient, including at the nurses' station or in the med room.
- If a tube is unlabelled when it reaches the lab, do not attempt to identify it from the requisition or from memory. Reject it.
- Barcode labels must be placed along the tube, not over the cap or wrapped around the tube in a way that obscures the fill line.

## Common rejection reasons

| Reason | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Unlabelled or mislabelled | Reject. Do not relabel. |
| Labelled but no second identifier | Reject and ask for recollection. |
| Hemolyzed | Reject if hemolysis affects the ordered test (potassium, LDH, etc.). See the test-specific list in the lab manual. |
| Wrong tube / wrong additive | Reject. Some tests can be recollected on the correct tube; do not transfer to another tube. |
| Clotted (for anticoagulated tubes) | Reject if the tube was not mixed adequately. |
| Insufficient volume | Reject if the fill line is not met. |
| Leaking container | Reject and treat as a potential exposure. |
| Expired collection tube | Reject. |

## How the lab tells the unit

- The receiving tech marks the specimen as rejected in the EHR. This generates a task to the ordering location and a notification to the collector.
- For urgent or stat orders, the lab also calls the unit or the paging system if the collector is not immediately reachable.
- The rejection reason is recorded in the EHR and visible to nursing and providers.
- Units can see a list of recent rejections in the EHR's laboratory worklist. The laboratory quality team reviews rejections at the monthly laboratory operations meeting.
- If the same unit or collector has repeated rejections, the Lab Quality Coordinator reaches out to the nursing leadership team for that campus to review the pattern.
