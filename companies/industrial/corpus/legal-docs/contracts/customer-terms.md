# Standard terms of sale

**Owner:** Legal  
**Last edited by:** Hugo Martins (Senior Counsel)

---

**Q: Where do the current templates live?**

The master copies are in the contract register under "Sales – Standard Terms". We keep three variants:

- **DE master** – used for the GmbH and for direct sales into Germany/Austria/Switzerland.
- **UK master** – used by Kessendorf UK Ltd for drives and distribution sales.
- **US master** – used by Kessendorf Inc.; the liability section differs materially from the DE/UK versions.

All three are maintained by the Legal team. If you need a copy, pull it from the contract register rather than forwarding an old PDF from a deal file.

**Q: Which version do I send to a customer?**

For a German customer, send the DE master. For a UK customer, send the UK master. For a US customer, send the US master. For a customer outside those three, use the DE master unless the entity is contracting with the UK or US legal entity, in which case use that entity's master.

If the customer insists on their own purchase terms, send them our terms anyway and ask them to mark up. We do not sign customer purchase terms without Legal reviewing the mark-up first.

**Q: The customer wants a higher liability cap. What can I agree to?**

The standard cap is the contract value, and that is the fallback position. For the GmbH, the cap is statutory in parts (product liability cannot be capped), but for the rest, the cap is negotiable up to twice the contract value if the account is strategic and the deal otherwise gets stuck. Above that, it needs Group General Counsel sign-off (Viktoria Hahn). The US master already has a different cap structure – check the section on "Limitation of Liability" before promising anything to a US customer.

**Q: They want a longer warranty than 12 months. What do I tell them?**

The standard warranty is 12 months from commissioning, capped at 14 months from delivery. For motion controllers and laser encoders, we can extend to 24 months on the controller/encoder only, not on the drives or direct-drive units. For TX series torque transducers, the calibration interval is 12 months anyway, so an extended warranty does not cover recalibration – make that clear in writing.

Anything beyond 24 months needs Legal to look at the deal. We have done 36-month terms for semiconductor-equipment builders where the tool is qualified once and then runs for years, but those are exceptions and always carry a condition that the customer follows the maintenance schedule in the manual.

**Q: The customer wants us to cover consequential loss. What is our position?**

We do not accept consequential loss. The standard wording excludes it, and we hold that line. The usual pushback is the customer saying "then the warranty is worthless if our line stops". The answer is that the terms include a repair-or-replace remedy with a reasonable response time, and that is what they are buying. If the customer needs cover for lost production, they need their own business interruption insurance.

The only area where we have accepted a form of consequential liability is for retrofits where the customer's own machine is down while we work on it – there we can agree to a liquidated-damages clause per day beyond the agreed commissioning date, but only with Legal's sign-off and only up to a fixed amount in the order.

**Q: We are doing a custom firmware build for a customer. Who owns the firmware?**

Our standard position: the customer gets a perpetual, non-exclusive licence to use the custom firmware on the hardware we supplied, but we retain ownership of the code and the right to reuse it for other customers. The firmware repository is ours.

Customers sometimes ask for exclusive ownership, and we decline. The business case for custom firmware only works if we can amortise the development across the product line. If the customer really needs exclusivity, that is a separate development agreement, not a sales term – escalate to Legal and we will draft one with a development fee that reflects the exclusivity.

**Q: What about IP in the motion control profiles or the measurement algorithms?**

Same answer as the firmware: licence to use, not ownership. The customer is buying the right to use the profiles and algorithms on the hardware we ship. If the customer has supplied their own algorithms and we integrate them, then their IP stays theirs and ours stays ours. The integration layer is ours.

**Q: The customer wants to buy through a distributor but have us invoice them directly. Can we do that?**

No. If the distributor takes title, the distributor is the customer of record and our terms apply to the distributor. If the end customer wants to be invoiced directly, the distributor would be invoicing on our behalf, and that changes the liability structure – we carry the end-customer risk. That only works with a formal agency agreement in place, and we do not set those up case by case. Refer the distributor to the sales team and we will sort out the right structure.

**Q: How do I send a mark-up to Legal for review?**

Upload the customer's mark-up as a PDF or {{WORD_PROCESSOR}} file into the contract register and create a change request against the relevant master. Add a note saying which deal it is, who the account manager is, and what the commercial pressure points are. We pick those up within two working days. If the mark-up is urgent, flag it in the change request and also post in #legal – but the request in the contract register has to exist first, otherwise we have no record.

**Q: The customer has marked up the liability cap and the warranty and the consequential loss. Which ones can we concede in one deal?**

Consequential loss is the one we hold. The cap and the warranty length are tradeable against price. If the customer wants all three, the deal needs Group General Counsel and the sales director to sign off on the commercial exception. We have done it, but it is a board-level decision and it will take time.

**Q: Do we have a version of the terms for the calibration lab?**

The lab uses its own service terms, not the sales terms. The lab's calibration certificates are governed by the accreditation body's requirements, and the service terms for calibration work are in the contract register under "Calibration Services – Terms". Do not send the sales terms to a calibration customer.
