# Credentialing and network status

**Owner:** Provider services  
**Last edited by:** Yasmin Lombardi (Provider Network Specialist)

## Initial credentialing

- [ ] Provider submits a completed credentialing application through the provider portal; paper applications go to Provider services and are scanned into the credentialing system by the records team.
- [ ] Application must be received at least 45 days before the requested effective date for network participation. Late submissions go to the next committee cycle.
- [ ] Provider services runs primary source verification:
  - [ ] Medical license (state licensing board for Indiana, Michigan, or other state where the provider practices)
  - [ ] {{DRUG_ENFORCEMENT_AGENCY|code}} registration
  - [ ] Malpractice coverage (current certificate, minimum limits per the Kellsbrook Health Plan provider agreement)
  - [ ] Board certification (if the provider claims board certification)
  - [ ] Work history and education for the past five years
  - [ ] {{PUBLIC_HEALTH_INSURANCE_1}}/{{PUBLIC_HEALTH_INSURANCE_2}} sanctions check
- [ ] Sanctions check must be repeated within 30 days before the committee review; a clean initial check is not enough.
- [ ] Hospital privileges are verified for providers who practice at a Kellsbrook hospital. If privileges are pending, the file goes to the committee with a note and is flagged as conditional.
- [ ] The credentialing committee meets twice a month. Members are the Provider Network Specialist team lead, the Medical Director (or designee), the Director of Clinic Operations for clinic-based providers, and a Health Plan representative.
- [ ] Committee decisions are recorded in the credentialing system. A denial or an approval with conditions is communicated to the provider in writing through the provider portal.
- [ ] Approved providers are loaded with their network status in the claims adjudication system on the effective date. The load is done by Provider services; claims pricing picks up the status automatically once the load completes.

## Recredentialing

- [ ] Recredentialing runs on a 36-month cycle. The credentialing system generates the list of providers due in the next 60 days.
- [ ] Providers are sent a recredentialing application through the provider portal 90 days before their current credential expires.
- [ ] Primary source verification is repeated for license, {{DRUG_ENFORCEMENT_AGENCY|code}}, malpractice, and sanctions. Board certification is rechecked if it has lapsed.
- [ ] If a provider does not respond by 30 days before the expiration date, Provider services contacts the practice manager or office contact by phone and follows up in writing.
- [ ] Providers who do not complete recredentialing are moved to a pending network status on their expiration date. Claims for services after that point price as out-of-network until the file is completed and approved.
- [ ] A provider who recredentialed late is not backdated. The new effective date is the committee approval date.

## Network status and claims pricing

- [ ] Network statuses in the claims adjudication system are: participating, pending, conditional, and non-participating. The provider portal shows the status to the provider.
- [ ] Pending status is used for approved providers whose effective date has not arrived and for recredentialing files that are past due.
- [ ] Conditional status is used for committee approvals with conditions (for example, practice location limitations or a shorter recredentialing cycle). The conditions are entered as notes in the credentialing system and visible to the claims team.
- [ ] Provider services owns the network status fields. The Health Plan does not change network status directly; a request from the plan goes through the Provider Network Specialist team.
- [ ] When a provider leaves a practice or terminates participation, Provider services updates the status to non-participating with the termination date. The update runs after the business day closes and claims are priced accordingly from the next working day.
- [ ] If a provider has multiple locations, the network status is the same across locations unless the committee sets a location-specific condition.

## Files and records

- [ ] The credentialing file is the system of record. Paper originals are scanned into the credentialing system and the paper goes to offsite storage.
- [ ] Provider services keeps a working file for each provider in the credentialing system with the application, verification results, committee notes, and correspondence.
- [ ] Access to the credentialing system is limited to Provider services, the credentialing committee, and Health Plan compliance. Read-only access can be requested through tickets by Revenue cycle for claims questions.
- [ ] Files for providers who were not approved or who withdrew are kept for the same retention period as approved files.
