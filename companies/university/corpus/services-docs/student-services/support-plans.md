# Disability support plans

**Owner:** Wellbeing & Disability Support  
**Last edited by:** Amara Ramos (Mental Health Adviser)

## Purpose

This page explains how a student's disability support plan is assessed, written, shared and reviewed. It is for anyone in Wellbeing & Disability Support who writes or updates plans, and for schools and the Exams Office who need to know how plans reach them.

## Assessment and writing

1. A student contacts Wellbeing & Disability Support or is referred by their school, Student Services, or the student casework system. The first conversation is usually with a Mental Health Adviser or a member of the Wellbeing team.
2. The adviser arranges a needs assessment. For most students this is a one-to-one meeting, either on the Hollins Hill campus or the City campus. Students at the Kuala Lumpur campus are assessed by video call.
3. The adviser checks what evidence the student has. We accept a diagnostic report, a letter from a consultant, or a summary from a previous university. If the student has no evidence, the adviser explains what they can get and offers to write to their GP or specialist.
4. The adviser drafts the plan in the student record system, under the Student Services tab. The plan has three sections:
   - **Inclusive teaching adjustments** – things the school does automatically (e.g. lecture slides in advance, recorded lectures).
   - **Assessment adjustments** – things the Exams Office or the school needs to arrange (e.g. extra time, rest breaks, a separate room).
   - **Non-academic support** – things like parking permits, accommodation adjustments, or signposting to other teams.
5. The adviser agrees the plan with the student. The student can ask for changes before the plan is finalised. We do not send a plan to a school until the student has confirmed it.
6. The adviser sets a review date. Most plans are reviewed annually, but some students need a review sooner (for example, if their condition changes mid-year). The review date is recorded on the plan.

## Sharing plans

7. Once the plan is finalised, the adviser marks it as "active" in the student record system. This moves the plan into the queue that schools and the Exams Office can see.
8. The school office gets a notification in the student record system. The school's disability contact (usually the senior tutor or the programme admin) is responsible for reading the plan and making sure teaching staff know what is in it. We do not email plans to individual lecturers unless the student asks us to.
9. The Exams Office is notified separately when a plan includes assessment adjustments. They pick this up from the student record system when they build exam timetables. If the plan is created during the exam period, the adviser also emails the Exams Office directly so they do not miss it.
10. For students on the Kuala Lumpur campus, the plan is shared with the Malaysia campus management team through the student record system in the same way. The KL campus has its own disability contact who coordinates with local teaching staff.

## Reviewing plans

11. Before the review date, the adviser emails the student to arrange a review meeting. This is usually a shorter conversation than the initial assessment.
12. At the review, the adviser checks whether the adjustments are working. The student may want to add, remove or change adjustments. The adviser updates the plan in the student record system and saves it as a new version.
13. If the plan is no longer needed, the adviser marks it as "closed" and records the reason. A closed plan stays visible to the student's school for the rest of that academic year, but is not carried into the next year.
14. Plans are not automatically renewed. If a student does not respond to review invitations, the plan stays active until the end of the academic year, then lapses. The student can ask for a new assessment at any point.

## If it goes wrong

- **A school says they never received the plan.** Check the plan is marked "active" and that the student's enrolment is current. If both are fine, ask the school to check the student record system again. If they still cannot see it, contact Student Systems via the IT service desk portal and include the student's ID.
- **The student says the plan is wrong.** Do not edit the plan in a rush. Ask the student what is wrong, make the change, and re-share the plan with the school so they get the updated version.
- **The Exams Office has not actioned an adjustment.** Email the Exams Office directly with the student's ID and the adjustment. Do not assume they have seen the plan – near exam boards the queue can be slow.
- **A plan needs to be urgent.** If a student has a sudden change in circumstances and needs adjustments within a few days, speak to Beatriz Dubois. She can authorise an interim plan that is shared with the school immediately, with the full assessment to follow.
