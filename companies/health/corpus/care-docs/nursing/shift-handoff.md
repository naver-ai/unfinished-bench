# Shift handoff (SBAR)

**Owner:** Nursing administration  
**Last edited by:** Theresa Okwuosa (Chief Nursing Officer)

---

## Before you start

- [ ] Review the patient's record in the EHR, including notes from the past shift, medication administration, and any outstanding orders.
- [ ] Confirm the patient's code status and any advance directives are current.
- [ ] Check the safety event reporting system for any open events involving patients on your assignment.
- [ ] Update the handoff tool in the EHR before you leave the unit — do not rely on memory or scrap paper.

## Bedside handoff — what to include

Use SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) at the bedside. Include:

- [ ] Situation: why the patient is here, current diagnosis, and what happened this shift.
- [ ] Background: relevant history, allergies, isolation precautions, and any recent procedures or transfusions.
- [ ] Assessment: current vital signs, pain score, mental status, lines/drains/airways, and any changes from the start of the shift.
- [ ] Recommendation: what the oncoming nurse should watch for, what is pending, and what needs to happen before the end of the next shift.
- [ ] Review medications due in the next two hours, including any that are held or refused and why.
- [ ] Confirm the patient's ID band matches the name and date of birth on the handoff tool.
- [ ] Introduce the oncoming nurse to the patient and family if they are present.

## High-risk patients

Flag these patients in the handoff tool and call them out verbally during handoff:

- [ ] Fall risk (use the current fall risk score from the EHR).
- [ ] Patients on continuous observation or with a behavioral health consult.
- [ ] Patients with a deteriorating early warning score — say the score and the trend.
- [ ] Patients with a new order for high-alert medications (heparin, insulin, opioids).
- [ ] Post-procedural patients still within the recovery window.
- [ ] Patients with a language barrier — confirm interpreter services are booked for the next shift if needed.

## Handoff between units and to procedural areas

- [ ] For transfers to another unit in the same hospital, complete the SBAR handoff in the EHR and give a verbal handoff to the receiving nurse before the patient leaves.
- [ ] For transfers to a procedural area (cath lab, endoscopy, OR), send the patient with the procedural handoff sheet from the EHR, not a printed chart copy.
- [ ] Call the receiving unit or procedural area before the patient is transported to confirm they are ready.
- [ ] For transfers between hospitals (South Bend, Valparaiso, Kalamazoo), the sending nurse must speak directly to the receiving nurse by phone — leaving a voicemail or a message with a unit clerk is not acceptable.
- [ ] If the patient is going to a procedural area at a different Kellsbrook hospital, also notify the scheduling and registration system so the receiving site has the correct encounter open.
- [ ] The oncoming nurse at the receiving unit is responsible for verifying the handoff tool was updated by the sending nurse. If it was not, they should contact the sending unit's charge nurse before accepting the patient.

## After handoff

- [ ] Both nurses sign the handoff tool in the EHR.
- [ ] The oncoming nurse does a quick room check: suction, oxygen, call bell, and bed alarm within reach.
- [ ] The outgoing nurse should not leave the unit until the oncoming nurse has had a chance to ask questions about the handoff.
- [ ] If you are the charge nurse, spot-check two or three handoffs per shift to confirm the tool is being used consistently.
