# Onboarding checklist (managers)

Owner: People team · Last edited by Gabriel Young (People Operations Specialist)

This checklist is for managers welcoming a new team member. Work through it with the new hire’s People Partner (Sam, Carlos, or Femke for escalations). Most account requests go through the IT service desk; open one ticket per item so each can be tracked and closed separately.

## Before day one

- [ ] Confirm start date and office (Rotterdam or Denver) with the People team at least two working weeks ahead.
- [ ] Submit the laptop order through the IT service desk. Include the role’s standard software bundle; add any role-specific requests (e.g., GIS plugins, notebook server access) in the same ticket’s notes.
- [ ] Request accounts via the IT service desk: company chat, email, calendar, VPN, and the password manager. List which internal systems the role needs (e.g., the imagery catalogue, the tasking portal, the customer API console, the helpdesk, the CRM, the data catalogue, the workflow scheduler, the notebook server, the BI dashboards).
- [ ] For roles that need warehouse or model serving access, add those to the same ticket.
- [ ] Check the access request policy in `handbook/it/access-requests.md` if you are unsure which systems need a separate approval.
- [ ] Assign a buddy from the team, ideally someone who started within the last year. Tell the buddy their role and point them to this checklist.
- [ ] Add the new hire to the relevant chat channels by name: their team channel, #announcements, #random, and either #rotterdam-office or #denver-office. Add #it-help and #ask-people as standard.
- [ ] Book a desk and any equipment (monitor, dock, headset) through the office coordinators. For Denver, note that desk booking runs through the same channel as Rotterdam, not a separate system.
- [ ] Add the start date to the team calendar so standups and planning sessions are covered.
- [ ] For Rotterdam starters, check with the sensor-network team whether a field visit slot is available in the first two weeks. They usually take one or two people per outing, so book early.

## First day

- [ ] Meet the new hire at reception (Rotterdam) or the lobby (Denver) and give them a short office tour: kitchen, meeting rooms, bike storage in Rotterdam, quiet floors in Denver.
- [ ] Make sure they can sign in to their laptop before anything else. If sign-in fails, walk them to the IT service desk or have them ping #it-help.
- [ ] Run through the team’s ways of working: where the team documentation lives, how standups are run, how work is tracked in the tickets system, and who to ask for what.
- [ ] Point them to the handbook README and to `handbook/company/about-brakwater.md` for the one-page overview. The org overview in `handbook/company/org-overview.md` is worth reading early too.
- [ ] Introduce them to their buddy and set a first check-in between them for the end of the week.
- [ ] Make sure they can see the imagery catalogue and the web app. Have them open both and confirm they can log in. If anything is missing, escalate the IT service desk ticket.
- [ ] Add them to the team’s shared calendar invites for recurring meetings. Do not assume calendar inheritance from the accounts request; it does not happen automatically.
- [ ] Send them the link to `handbook/people/working-across-offices.md` if their work involves the other office, and mention the overlap hours for meetings.

## First two weeks

- [ ] Schedule a 30-minute session with the new hire and yourself to go through the team’s current priorities and how their role contributes. Use the performance conversations guide in `handbook/people/performance-conversations.md` for structure.
- [ ] Confirm the buddy has had at least two informal check-ins. If not, nudge both sides.
- [ ] Have the new hire shadow a relevant recurring task: a support case review for customer-facing roles, a pipeline health check for data roles, a batch job submission for platform roles, an imagery ingest review for imagery-ops roles.
- [ ] For Rotterdam starters in field-adjacent roles (sensor-network, imagery-ops, applied-ml, support), confirm the field team visit is booked. The sensor-network team posts available slots in #sensor-network; coordinate there.
- [ ] Walk through the data catalogue and the warehouse overview so they know what data exists and where to find it. Link `data-docs/catalogue.md` and `data-docs/warehouse/overview.md`.
- [ ] Make sure they have read `handbook/security/acceptable-use.md` and the data classification notes. The People team will track completion; you just need to point them to it.
- [ ] Check in on how the accounts are working in practice: the notebook server, the workflow scheduler, the customer API console, whichever systems they use daily. Ask them to log any friction in the tickets system rather than working around it.
- [ ] At the end of week two, confirm with the People Partner that the onboarding ticket is closed and that any follow-up access requests are filed.

## Notes for managers

- Keep the first week light on meetings. Block out at least half of day one for reading and setting up.
- The buddy relationship is meant to last at least three months. Check in with the buddy around the two-month mark to see if the pairing still works.
- If the new hire is remote for the first weeks, adapt the office-specific items (tour, field visit) to a later date and make sure the buddy plans a virtual introduction instead.
- Ask the new hire to update the team’s documentation with anything they found unclear. That is how the checklist stays current.
