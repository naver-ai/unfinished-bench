# Records retention

**Owner:** Legal  
**Last edited by:** Luca Heller, Senior Legal Counsel

## Purpose

This policy sets out how long Mossgrove keeps different categories of records, and what to do when a legal hold applies. It applies to all staff who create, store or manage records on behalf of Mossgrove, including records held in company systems, on shared drives, in email, and in physical form at stores, the Wakefield DC, and the Leeds, Dublin and Columbus offices.

## Scope

The retention periods below are minimums. Some teams operate their own longer schedules — for example, finance keeps certain ledgers beyond the periods here, and the payroll team keeps its own calendar. Where a team's runbook says something longer, the longer period wins.

## Policy

1. **General principle.** Records are kept only as long as Mossgrove needs them for business, legal, regulatory or tax purposes, and are then deleted or destroyed securely. No one should keep records beyond the periods below without a business reason or a legal hold.

2. **Finance records**
   2.1. Supplier invoices, purchase orders and payment records: 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the transaction occurred.
   2.2. Customer sales records, including online orders, store transactions and wholesale invoices: 6 years from the end of the financial year of the transaction.
   2.3. Gift card ledger entries and unredeemed gift card balances: kept for the life of the gift card plus 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the last balance was redeemed or expired.
   2.4. Store banking records, cash reconciliation sheets and till rolls: 2 years from the end of the financial year, except where the store banking runbook says otherwise.
   2.5. Chargeback records (the acquirer's notices, the evidence pack as it was submitted, and the outcome), held in the dispute tracker: 6 years from the date the chargeback was resolved.
   2.6. Month-end close packs and working papers: 6 years.
   2.7. VAT records, including VAT returns and supporting calculations: 6 years, as required by {{TAX_AUTHORITY_1|code}} and Revenue.

3. **HR records**
   3.1. Personnel files for current staff: kept for the duration of employment plus 6 years after the person leaves.
   3.2. Payroll records, including the pay file and adjustments: 6 years from the end of the tax year.
   3.3. Leave and absence records: 3 years from the end of the leave year in which the absence ended.
   3.4. Expense claims and receipts: 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the claim was paid.
   3.5. Recruitment records for unsuccessful applicants: 12 months from the date of the decision not to hire, unless the applicant has given consent to keep their details longer.

4. **Customer records**
   4.1. Customer order history (online, store loyalty and wholesale): 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the last order was placed. Online accounts with no sign-in or order for six years are closed by the monthly dormancy job.
   4.2. Customer service and support tickets: 3 years from the date the ticket was closed. When a customer account is closed, the conversations attached to it are handled with the rest of the account (see `support-docs/account-closures.md`).
   4.3. Marketing consent records: kept while the customer remains on a marketing list and for 2 years after consent is withdrawn or the customer unsubscribes.
   4.4. Refund records: 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the refund was posted. Chargeback files are finance records and are covered by 2.5.
   4.5. Closed customer accounts: the order and payment records stay in the orders service's order archive, and the CRM record is kept, for 6 years from closure.

5. **Supplier records**
   5.1. Supplier onboarding documents, including bank details and due diligence checks: 6 years after the supplier relationship ends.
   5.2. Credit terms agreements and credit limit changes: 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the agreement was superseded or terminated.

6. **Legal holds**
   6.1. Legal issues a hold notice when records may be needed for a claim, a regulator's request or an investigation. The notice names the records, the teams and the systems it covers, and what those teams need to do while it is in place.
   6.2. Holds apply to the teams and systems named in the notice. Legal will confirm when a hold is lifted.
   6.3. If you believe records relevant to a hold may be about to be deleted automatically (for example by a scheduled clean-up), tell Legal or the helpdesk straight away.

7. **Secure destruction**
   7.1. Physical records are shredded or put in the confidential waste bins at the office or store. Hard drives and media are wiped by IT before disposal.
   7.2. Digital records are deleted using the system's normal delete function; where the system supports it, use the permanent delete option rather than moving to a recycle bin.

8. **Questions.** If in doubt about how long to keep something, or whether a legal hold covers it, ask Legal. The finance team can help with questions about the ledger periods in section 2, and the helpdesk can help locate records in the shared drives.
