# How support works

Developer support runs the support operation for Gantline Cloud, covering the community forum, email, and the enterprise priority queue. The team sits in both Lisbon and Portland and follows the sun so there is always someone on shift during working hours in each region.

Customers reach us in three ways. The community forum is the first stop for most questions — anyone can post, and the team plus other users answer there. Email comes into the helpdesk and creates a ticket automatically. Enterprise customers on the priority queue get a dedicated email address and their tickets are tagged so they surface at the top of the queue.

The helpdesk is our single system of record for all tickets, whether they arrived by email or were raised from a forum thread. Every ticket gets a priority based on the customer's plan and the impact described. First-response targets are tied to plan level: enterprise customers on the priority queue get a response within two working hours, business plans get a response by the end of the next working day, and team plans get a response within two working days. Free plans are supported through the community forum. These targets are measured from when the ticket is created, not when it is assigned.

Shifts are organised so Lisbon covers the morning and early afternoon, then hands off to Portland for the rest of the day. The overlap is short but deliberate — it gives us a chance to sync on anything that needs context. At the end of a shift, the person leaving adds a handover note to any open ticket that is not self-explanatory: what has been tried, what the customer is waiting on, and anything time-sensitive. Tickets that are waiting on the customer do not need a handover note unless the wait has gone on long enough that a fresh pair of eyes would help.

The Support Manager and the two Support Team Leads handle escalations, and the on-call engineer gets pulled in for service incidents. If a ticket looks like an outage or a widespread problem, the person on shift checks the status page and the #incidents channel before replying, and raises an incident if nothing is already open.

We keep a small set of internal docs that cover the common cases — escalations, enterprise onboarding, failed payments — and the team updates them as the product changes. If a fix is needed in Gantline Cloud itself, we file a ticket in the engineering tracker and link it to the customer ticket so the loop closes when the fix ships.
