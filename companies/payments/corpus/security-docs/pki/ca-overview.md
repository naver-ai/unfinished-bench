# Internal CA overview

The internal certificate authority is split into two tiers. The offline root CA is kept in a dedicated safe in the Dublin office and is only ever brought out for ceremony work — signing the issuing CA certificates or updating the root CRL. Everything else is signed by one of two issuing CAs, both of which live in the HSM cluster in Vilnius. One issuing CA is used for service and internal web certificates, the other for device certificates. The separation exists mostly so that a device certificate compromise does not put service mTLS at risk, and vice versa.

Certificates are issued for three broad purposes. Service certificates are used for mutual TLS between our own services, including the payments bus and the core ledger. Internal web certificates cover the back office, the merchant dashboard (the internal admin side), the treasury workstation, and the various internal tools that present a web UI. Device certificates are issued to staff laptops and to the YubiKeys used for signing and SSH. We do not issue certificates for external-facing endpoints; those go through the public CA process and are tracked separately.

The certctl service is the primary way to look certificates up. It indexes everything the issuing CAs have signed, so you can search by serial, by subject, by the SAN, or by the service name if one was recorded at enrolment. The web UI is at the usual internal certctl location and there is a CLI wrapper that most people use from their laptop. If you need to know whether a certificate is still valid, what it was issued for, or who requested it, certctl is the place. The HSM itself is not directly queryable; certctl is the front end for all of that.

CRL and OCSP distribution points are baked into every certificate we issue. The CRL is published from the issuing CAs and is also mirrored to the edge so that partners and external systems can fetch it without traversing the internal network. OCSP is served by a small cluster that sits behind the same edge path. If you are debugging a handshake failure and the error mentions revocation, check whether the client can reach the distribution points — the URLs are in the certificate and should be resolvable from both the Dublin and Vilnius networks.

Key sizes by certificate type are as follows. The offline root CA uses a 4096-bit RSA key. The issuing CAs in the HSM use 3072-bit RSA. Service certificates are issued with RSA 4096 keys; a few older service certificates were issued at 2048 bits and are replaced as they come up. Internal web certificates also use ECDSA P-256. Device certificates are RSA 2048, mostly because the {{SECURITY_KEY}} firmware on the older stock does not do ECDSA signing well and we have not finished replacing them. If you are requesting a certificate and the tooling offers a choice, use the default — it is set to the right key type for the purpose.

For more detail on the certificate lifecycle, replacing a service certificate is covered in [replacing a service certificate](../pki/replace-service-cert.md). Partner keys are a separate matter and are handled through [partner keys](../keys/partner-keys.md).
