# Safeguarding

**Owner:** Dean of Students & Student Casework  
**Last edited by:** Chidi Jovanovic, Student Caseworker

## Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Aldercombe University protects children and adults at risk who come into contact with the university through its teaching, outreach, research, and partnership activities. It applies to all staff, contractors, and volunteers, including those working at the Kuala Lumpur campus, Kestrel Vale Academy, and through Aldercombe Estates Services Ltd.

## 1. Who this policy applies to

1.1 All university staff, whether employed directly, through Aldercombe Estates Services Ltd, or engaged as contractors or volunteers.

1.2 Activities covered include: outreach events on either campus, work in schools, activity at Kestrel Vale Academy, residential summer schools, open days, and any event where under-18s are present. It also covers our contacts with students who are under 18 and with adults at risk, whether they are students, members of the public, or residents in university accommodation.

1.3 The university's three faculties and Professional Services teams must take reasonable steps to identify whether any participant in a university activity is under 18 or an adult at risk, and to make arrangements accordingly.

## 2. Designated safeguarding leads

2.1 The Dean of Students & Student Casework team holds the designated safeguarding lead (DSL) roles for the university. The current leads are Julia Rossi, Associate Dean of Students, and Enzo Kaplan, Head of Student Conduct. Chidi Jovanovic and Aaliyah Zhang, Student Caseworkers, are deputy leads.

2.2 The Headteacher of Kestrel Vale Academy is the DSL for the academy, with the academy's own deputy leads. Concerns about academy staff or the academy's safeguarding arrangements should be raised with the university's safeguarding leads in the first instance.

2.3 For the Kuala Lumpur campus, the campus director nominates a local safeguarding contact who works alongside the university leads. Concerns can be raised with either.

## 3. Reporting a concern

3.1 Anyone who has a concern about a child or adult at risk should report it the same working day to a designated safeguarding lead or deputy lead. Where the concern is urgent and no lead is available, contact the university's security team on either campus, who will escalate.

3.2 Reports can be made through the student casework system, by email to the Dean of Students & Student Casework team, or in person. If you are unsure whether something is a safeguarding concern, report it anyway.

3.3 Do not investigate the concern yourself. Do not promise confidentiality to the person raising the concern or to the person it is about. Record what you have seen or been told, and pass that record to the safeguarding team.

3.4 The safeguarding team will log the concern, decide on next steps, and keep the person who raised it informed of progress where appropriate.

## 4. Signs to look out for

4.1 Signs of abuse or neglect can include: unexplained injuries, changes in behaviour or attendance, withdrawal, fear of a particular person, inappropriate sexual behaviour, poor living conditions, or signs of financial exploitation.

4.2 For adults at risk, also look out for someone who appears to be under another person's control, who is unable to make decisions about their own affairs, or who is being pressured into decisions about their money, property, or care.

4.3 These lists are not exhaustive. If something does not feel right, raise it.

## 5. Students under 18

5.1 The university admits a small number of students under 18 each year, mostly students who start their course at 17. Student Records & Data flags these students on the student record system.

5.2 Staff who teach or supervise under-18 students should be aware of who they are in their classes. Schools and departments should make sure that activities involving under-18s have appropriate supervision arrangements and that any residential elements are planned with the safeguarding team's input.

5.3 Under-18 students are invited to consent to their parent or guardian being contacted about significant welfare matters. Where they do not consent, the safeguarding team decides whether contact is still needed.

## 7. Training and awareness

7.1 All staff complete the university's online safeguarding awareness module on joining, and refresh it every three years. The module is available through the VLE.

7.2 Staff who work directly with children or adults at risk, including academy staff and outreach teams, complete additional basic safeguarding training arranged through the Dean of Students & Student Casework team.

7.3 The safeguarding team offers drop-in sessions each term for anyone who wants to talk through a concern before deciding whether to report it formally.

## 8. Records and confidentiality

8.1 Safeguarding records are held in the student casework system and are restricted to the safeguarding team and, where relevant, Student Services staff who need to know. They are not shared outside the university except where required by law or by a statutory body.

8.2 Safeguarding records are kept in line with the university's data protection policy and retention schedule. If you hold any safeguarding information in a personal file, email, or other system, pass it to the safeguarding team and delete your copy.
