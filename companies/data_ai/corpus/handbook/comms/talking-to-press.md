# If a journalist contacts you

Journalists sometimes reach out to people on the sensor team after a flood, or to people in applied ML after a big crop failure story. If that happens, you don't need to figure it out on your own — just loop us in.

**Someone called me saying they're from a newspaper. What do I do?**
Ask for their name, outlet, and what they're working on. Then forward that to the Brand and communications team via chat (#marketing or just @Mirela Constantin) before you say anything else. Don't share data, imagery, or customer names.

**Can I at least tell them what I saw on the ground?**
You can describe what you personally observed at a sensor site or in the field in general terms — weather conditions, water levels you measured, that sort of thing. Keep it to your own experience, not company analysis or anything from the analytics API. If they push for numbers or maps, that's a comms question.

**What about saying "no comment"?**
That's fine if you're not sure. It's better than winging it. But even "no comment" should come after you've told comms about the call, because we may want to get back to the journalist with something useful before they file.

**A conference asked me to talk about our flood models. Is that OK?**
Talks and papers need a heads-up to comms first, even if the conference is academic or the talk is "just about the science." Send the abstract or outline to Brand and communications before you submit. Same goes for panel appearances or podcasts. We won't block things without reason — we just need to know what's out there.

**What if the journalist is from a trade publication about satellite data?**
Same process. Trade press still counts as press, and they sometimes have a specific angle on our tasking or pricing that you won't be able to answer anyway. Pass it along.
