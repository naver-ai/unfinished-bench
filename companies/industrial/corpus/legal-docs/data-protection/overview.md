# Data protection basics

**Owner:** Legal  
**Last edited by:** Delphine Esposito (Contract Manager)

---

## Group DPO and UK lead

- Group DPO sits in Reutlingen and reports through Legal to the Executive Board. Contact via the legal distribution list; do not route data-protection mail through the service desk portal.
- UK data protection lead is in Telford and covers UK processing, including the drives plant and the distribution centre. The US operation handles its own state-level matters and coordinates with the group DPO on cross-border transfers.

## Records of processing

- The register lives in the QMS as a controlled document set, one entry per processing activity. Each entry names the owning department, the systems involved (ERP, MES, CMMS, HR system, calibration database), the categories of data subjects, and the retention period.
- Department heads are supposed to review their entries annually. Owning departments that have not reviewed in over a year will be chased by Legal before the external audit window opens.

## Subject access requests

- SARs can arrive by post, through the intranet form, or via the general mailbox. The team that receives one should forward it to Legal within one working day. Do not ask the requester for ID until Legal has confirmed what is needed.
- Usual turnaround is 30 calendar days from receipt. Legal aims to respond inside 20 working days where the request touches a single system; requests spanning the ERP, time-recording terminals and the expenses app usually take the full window.
- If a request looks like it covers data held by more than one group company, Legal coordinates with the Telford lead and the US counsel before assembling anything. Rania Aoki handles the collection side from the mail archive and the file shares.

## DPIAs

- A DPIA is required for any new system or material change to an existing one that involves personal data. The checklist is part of the change and request tracker; the project lead raises the DPIA ticket in the tracker before build work starts.
- Legal reviews the DPIA and will sign off only when the technical controls are described concretely — naming the actual systems (e.g. the MES, the HR system, the calibration database) rather than "the platform".
- The test lab, the calibration database and the time-recording terminals are the usual repeat offenders. If the project team cannot name the retention periods in the DPIA, the review stops until they can.

## Data breaches

- Report any suspected breach to Legal via the QMS breach form. Include what happened, which systems were involved, how many records are affected, and what has been done so far. Do not wait for confirmation that a breach actually occurred.
- Legal decides whether the breach needs to go to the supervisory authority and whether affected individuals must be told. The group DPO has the final word on the wording of any notification.
- For breaches involving customer data, Legal informs the customer-facing teams so they can handle account-manager questions. The trade-compliance team is copied if the breach touches export-controlled technical data.
