# Reporting a security incident

**Owner:** Security (IT & product)  
**Last edited by:** Amara Lambert (Head of Security)

This page tells you what counts as a security incident and what to do when you spot one. If you are unsure whether something is an incident, report it anyway — that is what the service desk is for.

## What counts

Typical incidents include:

- Phishing emails, suspicious links, or someone asking for your password over chat or phone
- A lost or stolen laptop, phone, or access badge
- A USB stick or other device found on a shop floor, in a lab, or anywhere it should not be
- A motion controller, drive, or measurement instrument behaving strangely — for example, a DU series unit responding to commands it should not, or a TX series transducer producing readings that change on their own
- Someone you do not recognise in a restricted area, or a colleague's badge being used at odd times

## How to report

1. If you are on a Kessendorf device, open the service desk portal from the intranet and choose **Security incident** from the category dropdown. If you cannot reach the portal, email **security@kessendorf.com** instead.
2. In the description, include: your name, your site (Reutlingen, Telford, or Greenville), when you noticed it, and what you saw. Do not click links or open attachments from a suspicious email before you report it.
3. If the incident involves a physical item (lost laptop, found USB stick), keep the item if it is safe to do so and note where it was found. Do not plug the USB stick into any computer.
4. Send the report. You do not need to wait for details you do not have — a short entry is fine. The system will open a ticket and copy the security team.

## What happens next

The ticket lands with Security (IT & product). Lucia Lopez or another analyst will triage it, usually within a few working hours. Depending on what it is, they may:

- Ask you for more details by chat or a quick call
- Start a log review or pull data from {{ALERT_ROUTER|lower}} or the MES
- Involve the relevant plant team (for example, Manufacturing Engineering in Reutlingen or Greenville, or the Telford drives line team)

You will get an update on your ticket. There is no need to chase — the team closes the loop when the issue is understood or resolved.

## Do not investigate it yourself

Do not try to trace the phishing sender, test the found USB stick, or take a controller offline to see what it does. You may miss something or make it worse. If a machine is behaving strangely, follow the normal plant rules for stopping the line and then report it. The security team will take it from there.

## If it goes wrong

If you already clicked a link or entered credentials before you realised, say so in the report — the analyst will walk you through what to change and what to watch for. If you are on a shop floor and the situation is unsafe, use the EHS reporting tool first, then report the security side once you are out of the area.
