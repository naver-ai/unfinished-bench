# Quarterly merchant reviews

**Owner:** Merchant success (Europe)  
**Last edited by:** Rebecca Heller, Merchant Success Manager

## Purpose

The quarterly merchant review is our structured check-in on the health of the accounts we manage. It is not a sales conversation and it is not a support ticket review — it is a business review of how the merchant is performing on our platform and whether the relationship needs any intervention before it becomes a problem.

## Preparing a review

Pull the following from the back office and the analytics warehouse before the call:

- **Volumes** — processed volume and transaction counts for the quarter, split by card brand and by channel (card-present, e-commerce, recurring). Compare against the previous quarter and the same period last year.
- **Authorisation rates** — overall approval rate and the breakdown by decline reason code. We care about the top three decline reasons; if a merchant's rate has moved more than a couple of points, look at whether it is issuer-side or something in their integration.
- **Disputes** — chargeback counts and ratios, plus the dispute win rate. Flag anything trending toward the thresholds in risk-docs/risk/chargeback-monitoring.md.
- **Fees** — invoice totals and whether the merchant is landing where we would expect given their plan and pricing. Check the billing view in the back office for any adjustments posted in the quarter.
- **Settlement** — any failed payouts, settlement delays, or treasury exceptions. The treasury workstation has the payout history; the reconciliation tool will show breaks.

## Running the review

Reviews are a conversation, not a presentation. Use the numbers as the agenda but leave room for the merchant to talk about their business first — their pipeline, their plans, the markets they are entering. The numbers make sense in that context.

Standard agenda:

1. Merchant's business update (5–10 minutes)
2. Volume and authorisation trends (10 minutes)
3. Disputes and risk flags (5 minutes)
4. Fees, invoicing, and billing questions (5 minutes)
5. Product and platform roadmap — what is coming that affects them (5 minutes)
6. Open follow-ups from the previous review

For merchants in the freight and logistics segment, coordinate with Esther if the account is in her book — she has the segment context and can join the call where it is useful.

## Follow-ups

Every review produces at least one follow-up. Log it in the CRM the same day with an owner and a due date. Common follow-ups:

- Opening a ticket with the payments platform team for an integration issue the merchant has been working around
- Introducing the merchant to the partner programme team if they are asking about embedding payments
- Passing a product gap to Marco's team via the merchant apps backlog — do not promise timelines
- Scheduling a walkthrough of the merchant dashboard features they are not using

If a follow-up is still open at the next review, say so in the CRM note. Recurring open items get escalated to the team lead.

## Health score

After each review, update the health score field in the CRM. The score is 1–5:

- **5** — growing, low disputes, no open issues
- **4** — stable, minor follow-ups
- **3** — some concern (volume dip, disputes rising, billing questions unresolved)
- **2** — intervention needed this quarter
- **1** — at risk of leaving or being closed

A score of 2 or 1 triggers a review with the team lead and, where relevant, a conversation with risk via the case tool. The score is also used in the quarterly segment report that Sofia reviews.

## Renewal notes

For merchants on annual plans, use the review to capture renewal context in the CRM: their satisfaction level, any contract changes they have mentioned, and whether they are evaluating competitors. Do not discuss pricing changes in the review — that goes through Anders or the account manager who owns the commercial relationship. The CRM note should be enough for the renewal conversation to start without re-interviewing the merchant.
