# Room allocations

**Owner:** Student Accommodation  
**Last edited by:** Chiara Blanco  

This covers how rooms are allocated for the academic year, including the first-year guarantee, returning students, room swaps, adjustments, the waiting list, and keys/check-in. For hall-by-hall details (room types, floor plans, pricing bands), see [residences.md](residences.md).

## First-year guarantee

1. The guarantee applies to new undergraduates who firmly accept a place and apply for accommodation by the published deadline (usually three working days after A-level results week). It doesn't cover direct-entry students or those who come through Clearing.
2. Allocations Officers (Ibrahim's team) run the first-year allocation in batches from the student record system. The allocation uses the accommodation booking system, pulling in the applicant's campus, faculty, and any adjustment flag.
3. Rooms are offered by campus preference first (Hollins Hill, then City), then by distance from home. We don't allocate on a first-come, first-served basis.
4. Offers go out by email with a link to accept. Students have five working days to accept; after that the offer lapses and the room goes to the next person on the list.

## Returning students

1. Returning students apply through the accommodation booking system during the reapplication window (usually late in the spring term).
2. Rooms are allocated by a points system: continuing students with a clean conduct record and no outstanding debts get priority. Points are calculated automatically but Ibrahim's team reviews the list before offers go out.
3. We don't guarantee a room to returning students, but we do guarantee an offer to anyone who applies by the deadline and has lived in halls for at least one full year.
4. Room moves for returning students are handled as transfers, not new allocations — see room swaps below.

## Room swaps

1. Students can request a swap through the accommodation booking system. Swaps are only considered after the first two weeks of term, once everyone is in.
2. Ibrahim's team reviews swap requests once a week. Swaps between halls are allowed if both students agree and the rooms are in the same price band — otherwise one student pays the difference.
3. Swaps involving a change of campus need approval from Chiara, as they affect the residence life coverage on each site.
4. Emergency swaps (e.g. moving someone closer to a carer or away from a noisy neighbour) go to the front of the queue. Ada Andersen coordinates these with Residence Life.

## Adjustments for disabled students

1. Students who need a specific room type or an en-suite room because of a disability or long-term condition apply through the accommodation booking system and select the "accessibility adjustment" option.
2. Beatriz Dubois's team (Wellbeing & Disability Support) reviews the evidence and sends the adjustment to Student Accommodation. The adjustment is recorded on the student's record in the accommodation system.
3. Adjustments are applied before the main allocation runs. If a student's required room type isn't available, we hold their application until one comes up — they're not offered a standard room as a fallback unless they agree.
4. Adjustments carry over year to year, but students need to re-confirm them each year. If their needs change mid-year, they contact Wellbeing & Disability Support and a new adjustment is added.

## Waiting list

1. Anyone who misses the deadline or doesn't get an offer can join the waiting list in the accommodation booking system.
2. The waiting list is ordered by date of application, then by distance from home. Adjustments and students with a confirmed offer but no room (e.g. they accepted after the allocation ran) go to the top.
3. Ibrahim's team reviews the waiting list daily during August and September, and weekly after the start of term.
4. Waiting list offers expire after two working days.

## Keys and check-in

1. Keys are issued from the Residences Office on each campus. Joy Navarro's team handles the key inventory and the check-in desk rota.
2. Check-in runs from mid-morning to late afternoon on the first two days of the academic year, then normal office hours. Students who arrive outside these times use the after-hours line.
3. Students must bring photo ID and their offer email. If someone else collects a key on their behalf, they need written authorisation and a copy of the student's ID.
4. Key returns at the end of the year go through the same desk. Lost keys are charged to the student's account.

## If it goes wrong

- Allocation run fails or throws an error: contact Student Systems via the IT service desk portal. Don't re-run the allocation yourself — it can duplicate offers.
- A student is offered a room that's already occupied: mark the offer as void in the accommodation booking system immediately, email the student, and log an incident with the IT service desk. Check the room status in the system before re-offering.
- An adjustment is flagged but the student hasn't submitted evidence: contact Wellbeing & Disability Support to confirm the status before allocating.
- Waiting list order looks wrong: check the timestamps on the application — if it's a data issue, log it with Student Systems and note the correct order in the ticket.
- A student disputes their points for a returning allocation: Ibrahim's team reviews the calculation manually and changes it only if the system's input was wrong.
