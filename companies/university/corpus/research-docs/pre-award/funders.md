## UK Research Councils

**Typical cadence:** Most councils run two or three standard calls per year, usually with a fixed closing date. Responsive-mode (investigator-led) routes stay open all year but panel dates cluster. Check the council portal for each round rather than relying on the mailing lists — the lists lag by a week or more.

**Quirks:**
- {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_1|code}} and {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_4|code}} both expect the "impact summary" in plain English; the panel reads it first. We have had bids returned without review for using technical jargon in that section.
- {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_3|code}} and {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_6|code}} have separate eligibility rules for co-investigators from outside the UK. If a collaborator is based overseas, check their eligibility before costing — Laszlo has had to unpick two bids this year where the overseas partner didn't qualify for direct costs.
- {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_2|code}} is strict on the 18-month rule for resubmissions. If a bid was submitted to a {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_2|code}} call and rejected, it can't go back to the same call the following year. The pre-award team keeps a log — check with Tariq before promising a PI they can resubmit.

**RIS contact:** Tariq Lambert is the first point of contact for all council bids. Hana does the costing; direct queries about funder policy should go to Tariq.

**Loose ends:** We are still waiting to hear whether the new "open access block grant" arrangements affect how we cost gold OA in bids. Hana is monitoring the council guidance and will flag changes at the research office meeting.

## Medical and environmental charities

**Typical cadence:** These are less regular than councils. {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_11}} has moved to its own portal and runs continuous application routes for most schemes, with a couple of fixed-round schemes each year. {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_13}} and {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_14}} both run a spring and an autumn round. The environmental charities ({{RESEARCH_FUNDER_9|code}}, {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_10|code}}, the larger trusts) tend to be once a year, usually with a short window — six weeks from call to deadline is common.

**Quirks:**
- {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_11}}'s "outbreak" funding is always urgent and always has a compressed turnaround. We've had two requests this year where the PI needed a full costed bid in under two weeks. The costing team can prioritise, but the institutional approval still needs to go through the normal route — start that early if a PI mentions a fast-track call.
- Several of the older charities (including BHF) still want a paper signature on the final submission form. The PDF goes to the funder by post; factor in two working days for delivery.
- The big medical charities are all signed up to the Concordat on open access, but their policy pages are not always current. If a charity's policy is unclear, check with the library team before costing in OA.

**RIS contact:** Tariq Lambert for medical charities; Bruno Keller for the environmental trusts. Bruno has the relationship with the {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_11}} regional office.

**Loose ends:** The Levett Trust (a small regional funder) changed its contact person and we haven't had a reply to an email sent through the old address. Ida may know someone through procurement if we need a warm intro.

## {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_16}}

**Typical cadence:** Call topics open on roughly a yearly cycle, but individual topics have staggered deadlines. The work programme is published in advance; we keep a spreadsheet of relevant topics per faculty.

**Quirks:**
- The UK association remains subject to the grace period rules — if a call deadline falls in a period when the association is not confirmed, we can still submit but the grant agreement may be delayed. We advise PIs to factor this into their project start dates.
- The participant portal requires all organisations to have a PIC number and a signed mandate. Aldercombe's mandate is with the finance team; if a new school or institute joins a bid, they need their own PIC and the mandate needs updating.
- Costing for Horizon bids uses a different salary costing model to the research councils; Hana has the spreadsheet. Do not let a PI use the {{RESEARCH_FUNDER_8|code}} template for a Horizon budget — it won't map across.

**RIS contact:** Laszlo Schmidt handles the contractual side; Tariq handles the submission. Hana does the costing.

**Loose ends:** The old online submission handbook we link to from the research intranet has moved — the new portal has its own help pages. Someone should update the link in the pre-award pages when they get a moment.

## Industry sponsors

**Typical cadence:** No fixed pattern. Most industry approaches come through the Innovation and Knowledge Exchange team or directly to a PI. We see a cluster of new enquiries around the start of the financial year when companies have fresh budgets.

**Quirks:**
- Industry contracts are almost always subject to negotiation on IP and publication rights. The Research Contracts team (Laszlo) must be involved before any cost is shared with the sponsor — we have had a company use a draft costing to push back on the final price.
- Some large corporates have their own supplier onboarding requirements before they will issue a purchase order. This can add four to six weeks. If a PI mentions a company name, check with Ida in Procurement whether they are already set up as a supplier.
- Payment terms for industry are usually 30 days from invoice, but some funders ask for delivery milestones before they will release payment. The post-award team needs to know about milestone billing at the costing stage so the cash flow works.

**RIS contact:** Bruno Keller for any industry bid above the institutional approval threshold; Laszlo Schmidt for contract questions.

**Loose ends:** We still owe the Innovation and Knowledge Exchange team an updated list of which industry call deadlines we are tracking — Bruno was going to send that over but it hasn't happened yet.
