# Acceptable use

**Owner:** Security engineering  
**Last edited by:** Kasia Vogel (Senior Security Engineer)

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Mossgrove systems and accounts may be used. It applies to all staff, contractors, and anyone else issued with Mossgrove credentials or access to company systems. The goal is to keep customer data, company data, and the systems that hold them safe while allowing people to do their jobs without unnecessary friction.

## Scope

Covers all company-provided devices, accounts, and systems, including but not limited to: email, chat, the CRM, ledgerctl, the analytics warehouse, the POS back office, the wholesale order system, store tills, and any system reachable through the corporate network or VPN. It also covers work done on personal devices where those devices are used to access Mossgrove systems.

## Policy

1. **Accounts and authentication**
   1.1. Accounts are issued to named individuals. Do not share accounts, passwords, or multi-factor authentication codes with anyone, including colleagues. If you need access for someone else, request it through the access request process in the handbook.
   1.2. Where multi-factor authentication is available, you must use it. Exceptions are only for service accounts that cannot support it; those are managed by Security engineering.
   1.3. If you believe your account or credentials have been compromised, report it via the incident reporting process immediately and change your password.

2. **Acceptable use of company systems**
   2.1. Company systems are provided for work purposes. Incidental personal use is acceptable if it does not interfere with your work, consume unreasonable resources, or risk data security.
   2.2. Do not use company systems to store, transmit, or process personal data outside the course of your normal duties. This includes customer data, colleague data, and any data covered by the data classification policy.
   2.3. Do not download, install, or run unauthorised software on company devices or systems. If you need a tool, request it through IT.
   2.4. Do not bypass or attempt to bypass security controls, including firewalls, content filters, or access restrictions. This includes using unapproved tunnelling or proxy tools to reach systems you would not otherwise be able to access.
   2.5. Do not connect unauthorised devices to the corporate network, including personal routers, switches, or wireless access points.
   2.6. Store systems and the POS back office are for authorised store operations only. Do not use them to look up customer or stock information unless it is part of your job.

3. **Company devices**
   3.1. Company-issued laptops and phones must be kept with you or stored securely. Report lost or stolen devices to IT and Security engineering as soon as you become aware.
   3.2. Company devices are monitored for security purposes. This includes network activity, login events, and access to sensitive systems. Routine monitoring is automated; individual activity is only reviewed where there is reason to investigate a potential issue.
   3.3. Do not remove company devices from the UK, Ireland, or US without prior approval from your manager, except on standard travel covered by the business travel policy.

4. **Email and communications**
   4.1. Use company email and chat for work communications. Be mindful that email and chat are not appropriate for sharing sensitive customer data unless using the approved mechanisms (e.g. encrypted attachments, secure file sharing through sharectl).
   4.2. Do not send company data to personal email addresses or personal cloud storage.
   4.3. External email should be clearly identifiable as coming from Mossgrove. Do not use personal email accounts for correspondence with customers, suppliers, or wholesale partners.

5. **Generative tools**
   5.1. Mossgrove-approved generative AI tools may be used for work where they help you be more effective. The current list of approved tools is available from Security engineering. If a tool is not on the list, do not use it for work.
   5.2. Do not paste customer data, personal data, or any information classified as internal or confidential into generative tools, even if the tool's provider states that data is not used for training. This includes order details, customer names and addresses, payment information, and internal financial data.
   5.3. Content generated by approved tools must be reviewed before it is used in customer-facing communications or internal documents. You are responsible for the accuracy of anything you publish.
   5.4. If you are unsure whether a specific use is acceptable, ask Security engineering.

6. **Data handling**
   6.1. Access data only where you have a legitimate work need. Do not browse customer records, financial data, or other sensitive information out of curiosity.
   6.2. When sending data outside Mossgrove, follow the sharing data outside Mossgrove guidance in data-docs. Use sharectl where it is available rather than emailing files.
   6.3. Store sensitive data on approved systems only. Do not keep customer data on local drives, USB sticks, or personal devices unless there is a documented business need and the data is encrypted.

7. **Reporting**
   7.1. If you see something that looks like a security issue — a suspicious email, unexpected system behaviour, or an error message that does not look right — report it through the incident reporting process. You will not be penalised for reporting in good faith.
   7.2. If you are unsure whether something is a security issue, report it anyway. Security engineering would rather triage a false alarm than miss a real incident.

8. **Enforcement**
   8.1. Breaches of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract. Where the breach involves criminal activity, Mossgrove may refer the matter to law enforcement.
   8.2. Questions about this policy should be directed to Security engineering.
