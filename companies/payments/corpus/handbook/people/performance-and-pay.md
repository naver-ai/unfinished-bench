# Performance conversations and pay reviews

**Owner:** People team  
**Last edited by:** Elias Mishra (People Operations Coordinator)

This page covers how performance conversations work at Kilbarra, how the annual pay review runs, how calibration works, what a promotion case looks like, and what to do if you want to raise a concern about your own review.

## Twice-yearly check-ins

Every employee has a check-in with their manager twice a year. These are the two formal moments when you'll discuss how things are going against your goals, what support you need, and what your plans are for the coming half. The check-ins are recorded in the HR system, and both you and your manager should add notes before the meeting so the conversation has a shared starting point.

Check-ins are not just a formality before the pay review. They're the place to raise things that are getting in the way of your work, to ask for stretch opportunities, and to talk about longer-term direction. If you're not sure what to bring, your manager should help you prepare — the People team has a short guide for managers on running a good check-in, and it's worth asking for it if you haven't seen it.

## The annual pay review

The pay review happens once a year, aligned to the financial year. It covers base salary adjustments and, for roles that participate, bonus and commission payments. The review uses performance from the previous full year, not just the most recent check-in, so it's worth keeping your own notes across the year rather than trying to reconstruct everything in the weeks before.

Managers prepare a short written summary for each person they manage, setting out what was achieved, how it was achieved, and where there are gaps. These summaries feed into calibration (below). After calibration, managers share the outcome with their team members in a one-to-one conversation. There's no set script for that conversation, but the expectation is that you hear the outcome, understand the reasoning, and have a chance to ask questions.

## Calibration

Before any pay decisions are finalised, managers meet in calibration groups by business area. The purpose is to make sure similar contributions are treated similarly across teams — so an engineer in payments platform isn't held to a different bar than one in treasury ops, and a merchant account manager in Dublin isn't compared against a different standard than a colleague in Austin.

Calibration groups are run by the People team, and a senior leader from the area chairs each one. Managers bring their written summaries and are expected to advocate for their people. The discussion is confidential, and managers are asked not to share what was said about other people's teams.

If your manager wasn't able to attend calibration in person, they'll still be expected to have submitted their summaries and to have been available for a follow-up conversation with the chair. That's rare, but it happens around travel, so don't be surprised if your manager mentions it.

## Promotion cases

Promotion is a separate process from the annual pay review, though the two sometimes happen at the same time. A promotion case is put together by your manager, with input from you, and needs to show sustained performance at the level above your current role — not just a good few months. The case is reviewed by the relevant senior leader and, for roles above a certain level, by a panel drawn from outside your team.

You don't need to wait for the review cycle to start a promotion conversation. If you think you're ready, raise it with your manager at any point. They may ask you to take on some work at the next level first, or they may agree you're already there. If your manager doesn't support a case, they should be clear about what would change their view.

## Raising a concern about your review

If you disagree with any part of your check-in or pay review, start by talking to your manager. Most issues are resolved that way — often it's a misunderstanding about what was considered, or something your manager didn't know about. If you're not comfortable going to your manager, or you've spoken to them and still feel the outcome was wrong, the People team runs a review process. You can contact any member of the team and ask for a review of your case; you don't need to go through your manager.

There's a time limit for raising a concern after you've heard the outcome of a review, so check the HR system or ask the People team if you're unsure. The People team will look at whether the process was followed correctly and whether the decision was reasonable given what was known at the time. They won't change a decision just because you disagree, but they will look again if something material was missed.

Promotion decisions are also open to review, with the same route.

## One more thing

The HR system has a self-service page where you can see your check-in history and the summary your manager wrote for your last review. It's worth logging in and checking that it looks right — a few people have found their notes were attached to the wrong review period, and it's much easier to fix that early than to untangle it later.
