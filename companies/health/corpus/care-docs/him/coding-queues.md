# Coding work queues

**Owner:** Health information management (medical records)  
**Last edited by:** Cecilia Esposito (Medical Coder)

## Inpatient queues

- **IP Discharged Not Final Billed (DNFB):** All inpatient charts with a discharge date must be coded and final billed within 3 working days of discharge. South Bend and Kalamazoo are the main volumes; Valparaiso is smaller but has the same target.
- Queue order: oldest discharge date first. Do not cherry-pick.
- Observation status stays in the outpatient queue, not here, even when the patient was in a bed.

## Outpatient queues

- **OP Same-Day / Clinic:** ED, obs, and clinic procedure charts. Target is 2 working days from service date.
- **OP Ancillary:** Imaging, lab, therapy. Lower priority than the ED queue; pull from these when the main queues are clear.
- **OP Surgical:** Hospital-based surgery at all three hospitals. These carry more queries and take longer; plan your day around them if they are sitting.

## DNFB meetings

- Daily huddle (South Bend time) with coding, HIM, and revenue cycle. Pull the DNFB report from the EHR before the huddle; be ready to say why anything is over target.
- If a chart is stuck waiting on a physician query, say so. Do not let it sit silently.
- Escalate to Arjun if the same physician has more than two open queries or if a chart has been waiting longer than a week for a response.

## Physician queries

- Query through the EHR's query tool. Do not call or page a physician to ask a coding question — it has to be in writing.
- Keep queries specific: one question, the relevant options, cite the source. If the record supports only one answer, do not query.
- Queries get a 48-hour window for a response. After that, follow up once via the tool. If still nothing, flag it in the daily huddle.
- Do not send a query if you can resolve it from documentation elsewhere in the chart. Queries are for genuine ambiguity, not convenience.

## Productivity

- Inpatient coders: target is 12-15 charts per day depending on complexity. Outpatient coders: 30-40 per day.
- These are targets, not quotas. Complex charts (trauma, multi-day stays, research patients) take longer — use the complexity flag in the EHR when you log your time.
- The coding dashboard in the EHR shows your personal queue and your billing lag. Check it at the start of your shift.
- If your queue is empty, pull from the overflow bucket in the EHR. Do not log idle time.

## Remote coders

- Remote coders use the VPN client to reach the EHR. The same queues and targets apply; the dashboard is the source of truth.
- If your VPN drops mid-chart, the EHR saves your place. Log back in and finish the chart before moving on.
- Remote coders are expected to be in chat during working hours. If you step away for a block, set your status.

## Escalation

- Arjun is the coding supervisor. Route anything you cannot resolve in the normal flow to him: duplicate MRNs, missing documentation that is not a query issue, coding edits that will not clear.
- If a chart has been in your queue for more than 5 working days, tell Arjun. He will rebalance.
- Rebecca handles the clinical documentation improvement side — if you see consistent documentation gaps from the same service line, mention it in the huddle and she will take it up.

## Tools and links

- The EHR's coding worklist is the primary queue view.
- The DNFB report lives in the revenue cycle reporting folder.
- Old coding queue spreadsheets in the shared drive are retired — do not update them, use the EHR.

## Notes

- Kalamazoo has a handful of research-related inpatient stays that come through with study flags. Code them the same way as any other chart; the study coordinator handles the research side.
- Cork does not route coding through this team. Their charts are handled locally and do not appear in these queues.
