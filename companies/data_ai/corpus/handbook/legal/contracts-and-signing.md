# Contracts and who signs them

Owner: Legal and compliance · Last edited by Liesbeth Kramer

This page covers the contract types we sign, how to request one, and who has signing authority. Everything in here applies to both Rotterdam and Denver unless stated otherwise.

## Requesting a contract

All contract requests go through the contract request form in the IT service desk. The form asks for the counterparty, the contract type, the expected value, and the business owner. Legal and compliance picks up new requests from that queue and assigns a paralegal or counsel to the file. If you need something signed within a working day, put that in the request notes — do not email individuals directly, it gets lost.

For customer MSAs and order forms, the sales team starts from the standard templates Legal keeps and works with Legal before sending anything out. Do not edit the template clauses yourself; mark suggested changes in the document and Legal will incorporate them.

## Where signed copies live

Signed contracts are stored in the contract register, one file per counterparty. Each contract gets its own record with the executed PDF, the final negotiated version, and any side letters. The paralegals keep the contract register up to date. If you need a copy of something we signed, ask Legal and compliance rather than digging through someone's drive.

## Things to watch

- Never sign an NDA on behalf of the company unless you are in Legal and compliance. The other side's NDA, even if it looks short, goes to Legal first.
- Order forms that reference a customer MSA we have not signed yet cannot be signed independently.
- If a supplier insists on their own terms instead of our standard purchase terms, the contract has to go through Legal before anyone signs.
- Research agreements always include the data handling annex even if the university says they do not need it. Mina Novak checks the DPIA notes before we sign.

## General rule

When in doubt, ask Legal and compliance before anyone signs anything. A signature from the wrong person is harder to unwind than a short delay.
