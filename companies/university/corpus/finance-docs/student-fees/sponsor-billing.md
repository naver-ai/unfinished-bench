# Sponsor billing

Owner: Student Fees & Accounts (the Bursary)  
Last edited by: Lucia Ferrante

1. Before a student enrols, the sponsor (employer, embassy, or other organisation) sends us a sponsor letter confirming they will pay all or part of the tuition fees. Email the letter to the Bursary inbox or upload it through the sponsor portal on the finance system.
2. Check the sponsor letter covers the full academic year. If it only covers one semester, raise it with the student and the sponsor before the first invoice goes out — we don't chase partial-year sponsors mid-year unless the letter says otherwise.
3. Set the student's fee record to "sponsor billed" in the finance system and add the sponsor's billing contact and purchase order number if they use one. If the sponsor is a UK employer, add their company registration number as well.
4. Invoices go out twice a year, after the finance system runs the semester billing batch. For the autumn semester, invoices are issued after the enrolment freeze; for the spring semester, after the module change deadline. The batch covers any sponsor who has a valid letter on file by the cut-off.
5. After the batch runs, check the sponsor billing report in the finance system for any students whose sponsorship was set up but who had no letter attached. Contact the student via their university email to ask for the letter — if it doesn't arrive by the payment due date, the student becomes liable for the fees and we reissue the invoice to them.
6. Sponsor invoices are due 30 days from issue. The finance system sends one reminder at day 14 and a second at day 28 automatically. After that, Lena's team (Credit Control) picks up chasing by phone or email.
7. If a sponsor has not paid by the end of the semester, send them a formal reminder letter from the Bursary, copying the student. If the sponsor says they will not pay, or stops replying, write to the student to tell them they are now liable for the outstanding amount and arrange a payment plan through the usual fees process.
8. When a sponsor stops paying part-way through a year, set the student's fee record back to "self-funded" from the date the sponsor confirms they will no longer pay. The student is then billed for the remaining semester(s) at the standard rate. If the student has already had a refund from an overpayment, Valentina on the Student Fees team can tell you how it was applied.

**If it goes wrong:** Sponsors sometimes pay late or pay the wrong amount. If a payment arrives that doesn't match an invoice, check the sponsor's remittance advice first — most mismatches are a part-payment or a payment covering multiple students. If a sponsor disputes a charge (for example, they say the student withdrew earlier than we recorded), ask Gabriel to pull the enrolment dates from the student record system and send the sponsor a copy of the enrolment confirmation.
