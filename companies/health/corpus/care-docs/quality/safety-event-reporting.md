# Reporting a patient safety event

**Owner:** Quality & patient safety  
**Last edited by:** Joy Balogun (Director of Quality & Patient Safety)

This covers what to report, how to enter it in the safety event reporting system, and what happens after you submit. Anyone can report — clinical or non-clinical, any site, any shift.

## What counts

Report anything that harmed or could have harmed a patient, including:

- Falls (with or without injury)
- Medication events — wrong drug, wrong dose, wrong route, omitted dose, adverse reaction
- Procedure or treatment events
- Equipment or device problems
- Transfusion or lab specimen events
- Patient identification errors
- Pressure injuries
- Near misses — anything that reached the patient but did not cause harm, or was caught before it reached the patient

If you are not sure whether it counts, report it. The Quality team would rather review a report that turns out to be minor than never hear about something that matters.

## Entering a report

The safety event reporting system is available from the clinical workstation menu and from the staff intranet on any Kellsbrook device. The workstation route is faster.

1. Open the safety event reporting system from the workstation menu (the same menu that opens the EHR and the lab information system).
2. Select **New report**.
3. Search for the patient by name or MRN. If the event did not involve a specific patient (for example, a damaged piece of equipment found in a hallway), leave the patient field blank.
4. Choose the **event type** from the list. If nothing fits, choose **Other** and describe it in the narrative.
5. Fill in:
   - Date and time of the event (use the actual event time, not the time you are entering the report)
   - Location (building, unit, clinic, or site)
   - What happened, in plain language
   - What you did after the event
   - Whether the patient was harmed, and if so, how
6. Attach photos or documents if you have them. Do not attach anything that contains another patient's information.
7. Select **Submit**.

You will get a confirmation number. Share that number if anyone follows up with you about the event. You can go back into the report later to add information, but you cannot delete a submitted report.

## What happens next

- The report goes to Quality & patient safety. Leon Singh or Gideon Horvath triages reports during normal business hours; the on-call quality person reviews urgent reports outside those hours.
- Minor events and near misses are reviewed by the unit or clinic manager and the Quality analyst assigned to that area. The reviewer may contact you for more detail.
- For serious events (death, permanent harm, or events that could have caused either), the Quality team starts a review. Marcus Whitcomb is notified for events that attract external attention.
- The reviewer decides whether a root cause analysis is warranted. If it is, a small team — usually the unit manager, a physician or pharmacist depending on the event type, a Quality analyst, and sometimes a representative from the site involved — meets within a few working days to go through what happened and what should change.
- You will not always hear the outcome of the review. That is normal. What matters is that the event is on record and the team is looking at it.

## Just culture

We do not use the safety event reporting system to punish people. Mistakes happen; our job is to understand why and make it less likely to happen again. Reports are not shared with managers for disciplinary purposes, and the system is not a substitute for reporting a crime, abuse, or deliberate harm — those go through the normal channels.

If you are involved in an event, report it yourself if you can. It is better for the review to hear your account directly than to reconstruct it later.

## If it goes wrong

- **You submitted the wrong patient or the wrong event type.** Open the report and correct it. If you already submitted, you can still edit the report until the Quality team closes it.
- **You forgot to include something.** Add an addendum through the report detail view.
- **The system will not load.** Call the IT service desk. If the system is down for more than an hour, write down the details and enter the report as soon as it is back. For a serious event while the system is unavailable, call the nursing supervisor or the site manager so they can escalate.
- **You are worried about retaliation.** Talk to Joy Balogun directly, or use the compliance hotline if you prefer to stay anonymous.
