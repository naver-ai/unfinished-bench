# Complaints handling

**Owner:** Compliance  
**Last edited by:** Soren Hughes, Head of Compliance & MLRO  
**Applies to:** All Kilbarra staff who interact with merchants, partners, or their end customers, and all teams that receive or process complaints.

## Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Kilbarra defines, records, and resolves complaints from merchants, partners, and their end customers. It covers acknowledgement and final response deadlines, referral rights, and the root-cause review process. It applies to complaints received through any channel — the helpdesk, the merchant dashboard, partner managers, email, phone, or social media.

## 1. What counts as a complaint

1.1 A complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction from a merchant, partner, or their end customer about Kilbarra's products, services, or staff, where a response is expected. This includes dissatisfaction with card acquiring, e-money balances, payouts, payment links, invoicing, or platform accounts.

1.2 An enquiry, a feature request, or a request for information is not a complaint, even if the tone is frustrated. If it is unclear whether something is a complaint, treat it as one.

1.3 If a merchant or partner uses the word "complaint" or says they want to escalate, it is a complaint. Do not reclassify it to avoid the complaints process.

## 2. Logging complaints

2.1 All complaints must be logged in the helpdesk with the "complaint" tag. The ticket must record the complainant's identity, the date received, the product or service involved, and a description of the issue.

2.2 If a complaint is received by phone or in person, the person receiving it must create the helpdesk ticket and record the details before the end of the same working day.

2.3 If a complaint arrives through a channel that does not create a helpdesk ticket automatically (for example a message to a partner manager or a social media post), the receiving team must create the ticket manually and link it to the merchant or partner record.

2.4 The helpdesk ticket is the single record of the complaint. All correspondence, notes, and decisions must be added to the ticket. Do not keep complaint details in personal mailboxes or separate spreadsheets.

## 3. Acknowledgement and final response

3.1 Acknowledge every complaint within two working days of receipt. The acknowledgement must confirm that Kilbarra is looking into the matter, state who is handling it, and explain the next steps.

3.2 Where possible, provide a final response within fifteen working days of receipt. If a full response will take longer, send an interim response explaining why and when a full response can be expected. Interim responses do not reset the fifteen-working-day clock.

3.3 The final response must address each point raised, state what Kilbarra will do (if anything), and, where the complainant is eligible, explain their right to refer the matter to the {{FIN_OMBUDSMAN_1}} ({{FIN_OMBUDSMAN_1|code}}). Referral rights must be stated clearly and in plain language, including the time limit for referring the matter.

3.4 For complaints from US merchants served through the sponsor bank programme, the final response must confirm whether the complaint has been resolved and explain any further rights under the sponsor bank's process. Coordinate with the US office before sending.

3.5 If a complaint involves a regulated payment service, the response must also state that the complainant may refer the matter to the {{FIN_OMBUDSMAN_1|code}} even if the complaint was partly or fully upheld.

## 4. Roles and routing

4.1 The helpdesk team triages all complaint-tagged tickets and assigns them to the relevant team — merchant billing, payments engineering, partner integrations, risk, or compliance — depending on the subject.

4.2 The assigned team owns the complaint through to the final response. They may ask other teams for input, but the assigned team remains responsible for meeting the deadlines in section 3.

4.3 The Payments Compliance Lead (Grace Osei) or the Compliance Manager (Ilse Dahl) reviews all final responses before they are sent. If a deadline is at risk, tell Compliance early so the review can be fitted in.

## 5. Root-cause review

5.1 Once a complaint is closed, the assigned team must complete a short root-cause note on the helpdesk ticket. The note should state what went wrong, why it went wrong, and what change (if any) will prevent a recurrence.

5.2 For complaints that involve a payment error, a failed payout, or a chargeback dispute, the assigned team must also share the root-cause note with the relevant engineering team through the tickets system, so that systemic issues can be tracked.

5.3 Compliance reviews closed complaints quarterly (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4) to identify themes — for example repeated issues with a particular product, merchant segment, or integration pattern. Findings are shared with the Merchant Payments team and, where relevant, with Risk & compliance.

5.4 If a complaint reveals a potential regulatory breach, a data protection issue, or suspected financial crime, do not wait for the quarterly review. Escalate immediately through the incident process and notify Compliance.

## 6. Training and awareness

6.1 All staff who may receive complaints — including support, account management, and partner integration teams — complete the complaints module in the learning platform as part of onboarding and then again when the module is updated.

6.2 The Compliance team publishes a short summary of complaint trends after each quarterly review, and it is shared in #compliance and #merchant-success. The summary does not include merchant names or identifying details.
