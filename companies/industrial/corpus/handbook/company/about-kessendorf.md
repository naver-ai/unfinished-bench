# About Kessendorf

Kessendorf Motion & Messtechnik GmbH started out in Reutlingen as a small encoder and precision-instrument workshop. The founders built optical encoders for machine tools and test rigs, and the company grew from there into complete motion-control systems — first the controllers, then servo drives and direct-drive units. Along the way we acquired a drives manufacturer in Telford, which became Kessendorf UK Ltd, and later opened Kessendorf Inc. in Greenville, South Carolina, to serve the Americas market. Today the group employs around 3,000 people across the three sites.

We organise our work around two business lines. **Motion** covers the MC-series motion controllers, the DU-series servo drives and direct-drive units, and the firmware and software that tie them together. **Metrology** covers the TX-series torque transducers, the laser encoders, and the calibration services that go with them. The two lines share engineering know-how more than you might expect — a torque transducer and a direct drive both come down to measuring and controlling small displacements with high precision.

Our customers are mostly machine builders in packaging, machine tools, semiconductor equipment, and test-rig construction. That shapes how we work: their machines run continuously, their tolerances get tighter every generation, and they expect a replacement drive or a calibrated transducer to arrive with the same paperwork every time. Field service and retrofits are a growing part of the business, particularly for packaging lines that were originally fitted with third-party drives.

The Reutlingen site is the headquarters and the main plant. It houses the test lab, the accredited calibration lab, and the bulk of the engineering and production for the Metrology line — torque transducers and laser encoders are assembled and calibrated here. Reutlingen also does final assembly for the MC-series controllers and hosts the executive board and most central functions.

Telford is the UK drives plant and the distribution centre for the UK and Ireland. The DU-series servo drives and direct-drive units are manufactured there, alongside a smaller line that assembles drives for retrofit projects. Telford also runs the UK distribution centre, which holds stock of drives, controllers, and spare parts for the UK service team.

Greenville is our US plant and the base for the Americas service organisation. The plant does assembly and testing of drives and controllers for the North American market, configured for local voltage and certification requirements. The Americas service team operates out of the same site, so customers in that region get repairs and calibration support without shipping equipment across the Atlantic.

The group companies work as one operating business in most respects. The ERP covers all three sites, and the shared systems — PLM for engineering, MES for the shop floor, CMMS for maintenance, QMS for quality — are used across the plants with local variations where the plant processes differ. The intranet is the best place to find which system to use for a given task; the handbook sections on IT access and the site pages give an overview of how the sites fit together.

If you need a quick orientation, the org overview in the handbook shows where teams sit, and the site pages have practical details about each location. For anything about the product lines, the marketing team in Reutlingen keeps customer stories and trade-fair materials that describe the applications in more depth than this document intends.
