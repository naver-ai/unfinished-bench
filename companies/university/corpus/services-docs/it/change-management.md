# Change management

**Owner:** IT Service Desk  
**Last edited by:** Eleni Hayashi (IT Service Desk Analyst)

## Purpose

This document sets out how changes to IT services and systems at Aldercombe are requested, reviewed, approved and implemented. It covers standard, normal and emergency changes, the weekly Change Advisory Board (CAB), and the freeze periods that apply around enrolment and results release.

## Scope

Applies to all changes to production IT services, including the student record system, the VLE, the finance system, the research grants system, the HR and payroll system, the timetabling system, the room booking system, the estates helpdesk, the lettings system, the supporter CRM, the library system, the expenses tool, the IT service desk portal, the accommodation booking system, the student casework system, the academy's school information system, and any other system or service managed by IT Services. Changes to business processes that do not involve IT systems are out of scope.

## 1. Change tickets

1.1 All changes must be logged as change tickets in the ticket system. A change ticket is distinct from an incident or a service request. If a piece of work turns out to be a change rather than a fix or a request, the ticket must be converted before work proceeds.

1.2 The change ticket must record: the system or service affected, the reason for the change, the expected impact, the rollback plan, the proposed implementation date and time window, and the name of the person requesting the change.

1.3 No change may be implemented without an approved change ticket, except as described in section 4.

1.4 The ticket must be updated as the change progresses. The person implementing the change is responsible for keeping the ticket current.

## 2. Standard changes

2.1 A standard change is a low-risk, pre-approved change that follows a documented procedure. Standard changes do not need to go to the CAB.

2.2 Examples of standard changes include: applying routine operating system patches to non-critical servers, adding a user to a system group as part of a role change, provisioning a new mailbox for a starter, and restarting a service outside core hours as part of a documented runbook.

2.3 For a change to be treated as standard, the procedure must already be documented and the change must fit the procedure exactly. If the change deviates from the documented procedure in any way, it must be treated as a normal change.

2.4 The IT Service Desk maintains the list of approved standard changes. Additions to the list are reviewed at the CAB.

## 3. Normal changes

3.1 A normal change is any change that is not standard and not emergency. Normal changes are reviewed at the weekly CAB.

3.2 The change ticket must be submitted at least five working days before the proposed implementation date. Tickets submitted later will be reviewed at the following CAB unless the submitter can justify a shorter lead time and the CAB chair agrees.

3.3 The CAB meets once a week. The meeting is chaired by the Director of IT Services or, in their absence, the IT Service Desk Manager. Attendance includes representatives from IT Services, the relevant faculty or professional service team, and, where the change affects their area, Student Records, Registry, Student Fees, Research Finance, Estates, or the Kestrel Vale Academy office.

3.4 The CAB reviews each change for risk, impact, and whether the proposed implementation window is appropriate. The CAB may approve, reject, or ask for more information. A change that the CAB sends back for more information must be resubmitted for a later meeting.

3.5 Changes that affect teaching spaces, student-facing systems, or the academy's school information system should be scheduled outside core teaching and school hours wherever possible. The CAB will normally ask for the implementation window to be adjusted if it conflicts with teaching or with key student deadlines.

3.6 Once approved, the change should be implemented in the window stated on the ticket. If the implementation needs to move to a different window, the ticket must be updated and the change returned to the CAB for confirmation unless the new window is within the same maintenance period and the risk profile is unchanged.

3.7 After implementation, the ticket must be closed with a note of what was done and any follow-up actions. If the change had to be rolled back, that must be recorded on the ticket with the reason.

## 4. Emergency changes

4.1 An emergency change is one needed to prevent or resolve a significant disruption to a service, or to address a security vulnerability that is actively being exploited.

4.2 Emergency changes may be implemented without prior CAB approval, but the change ticket must still be raised before implementation begins, and the IT Service Desk Manager or the Director of IT Services must be notified as soon as possible.

4.3 The notification can be by phone or in chat. The change ticket must be referenced in the notification.

4.4 Emergency changes are reviewed retrospectively at the next CAB. The review covers whether the change was genuinely emergency, whether the implementation was carried out safely, and whether any follow-up work is needed.

4.5 An emergency change does not remove the need to test the change or to have a rollback plan. If it is not possible to test before implementation, the ticket must say so and testing must happen as soon as possible after.

## 5. Change Advisory Board

5.1 The CAB considers changes from all parts of the university, including the Kuala Lumpur campus and the academy. Changes to systems hosted for Aldercombe University Malaysia are reviewed in the same CAB; where the change is specific to the KL campus, the KL property team or the KL campus office will be invited.

5.2 The CAB agenda and papers are circulated at least two working days before the meeting. Papers are the change tickets themselves; there is no separate submission form.

5.3 CAB decisions are recorded on the change tickets. A short summary of decisions is posted to the #it-services channel after the meeting.

5.4 Anyone in IT Services may raise a change for the CAB. Colleagues outside IT Services who need a change should contact the IT Service Desk or raise the request through their local IT contact.

## 6. Freeze periods

6.1 A change freeze applies from two weeks before the start of enrolment until the end of the second week of teaching in each semester. During the freeze, only emergency changes and changes that the CAB has approved as low risk and non-disruptive may be implemented.

6.2 A further freeze applies from the publication of the exam timetable for the main exam period until the end of the results release period. During this freeze, changes to the student record system, the VLE, and the exam-related systems are limited to emergency changes only.

6.3 Changes to the finance system that affect month-end or year-end processing should not be scheduled for the last three working days of any month, and the final two working days of the financial year are also subject to the same restriction.

6.4 The CAB maintains a calendar of freeze periods. The calendar is available on the IT services page and is updated as soon as dates are confirmed.

6.5 If a change is proposed during a freeze period, the ticket should be marked accordingly and the CAB will consider whether the change can wait. The default position is that non-emergency changes wait until the freeze ends.

6.6 Emergency changes during a freeze period follow the normal emergency process in section 4, but the notification must also go to the head of the affected service area in addition to the IT Service Desk Manager or Director of IT Services.
