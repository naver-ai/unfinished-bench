# Leave and absence

**Owner:** People & HR  
**Last edited by:** Claudia Rehm (Chief People Officer)

**Purpose.** This document sets out holiday entitlement, public holidays, sickness reporting and the main leave-of-absence types for Kessendorf employees in Germany, the UK and the US. It is the reference for managers and employees when planning or recording time away from work.

**Scope.** All permanent and fixed-term employees of Kessendorf Motion & Messtechnik GmbH, Kessendorf UK Ltd and Kessendorf Inc. Agency workers and interns follow the terms of their assignment agreement; where this document says "employee" below, it applies to them only if their contract says so.

---

## 1. Holiday entitlement

1.1 **Germany (Reutlingen).** Full-time employees receive 30 working days of holiday per calendar year. Part-time employees receive the pro-rata equivalent, rounded up to the nearest half day. Employees who join or leave part-way through the year accrue holiday on a monthly basis (2.5 days per full month of employment).

1.2 **UK (Telford).** Full-time employees receive 25 working days of holiday per leave year (which runs from the first working day of January), plus the eight bank holidays for England and Wales. Part-time employees receive the statutory minimum plus a pro-rata uplift, calculated by the HR system on appointment.

1.3 **US (Greenville).** Full-time employees receive paid time off (PTO) as follows, based on length of service at the start of the calendar year:
- 0–4 years: 15 working days
- 5–9 years: 20 working days
- 10 or more years: 25 working days

PTO is credited in full at the start of each calendar year. Part-time employees receive a pro-rata credit. PTO does not carry over beyond the calendar year except where required by state law; the HR system automatically forfeits unused days at year end.

1.4 **Carry-over.** Germany: up to 5 days may be carried into the first quarter of the following year with manager approval, recorded in the HR system as carry-over. UK: up to 5 days may be carried over with manager approval. US: no carry-over except where state law requires.

1.5 **Booking leave.** All holiday must be booked in the HR system before the leave is taken. Managers approve or reject requests in the system. For production and test-lab staff, requests should also be reflected in the MES shift plan; for warehouse staff, the dock plan is the reference. Where a request conflicts with an approved plan, the manager agrees an alternative.

1.6 **Notice periods.** Employees should give at least twice the length of the leave as notice (for example, five days' leave needs ten working days' notice), unless the manager agrees otherwise. The plant teams (Telford drives line, Greenville production, Reutlingen test lab) may set shorter notice for slots in annual shutdown weeks.

---

## 2. Public holidays

2.1 **Reutlingen.** Public holidays follow the Baden-Württemberg calendar. Where a public holiday falls on a non-working day, no replacement day is given. The works council calendar lists the exact dates each year; the HR system pre-populates them.

2.2 **Telford.** Public holidays are the eight bank holidays for England and Wales. Where a public holiday falls on a non-working day, the replacement day is set by the UK government and applied automatically in the HR system.

2.3 **Greenville.** Public holidays are the US federal holidays observed by the state of South Carolina. The plant publishes its holiday schedule each quarter; the HR system pre-populates them.

2.4 Employees who work on a public holiday (for example, shift coverage in the test lab or the outbound dock) receive a day in lieu, booked in the HR system as lieu time and taken within three months.

## 3. Sickness reporting

3.1 **Germany.** Employees must notify their manager by telephone or the chat channel #hr-ops by mid-morning on the first day of absence, stating the expected duration. From the fourth calendar day of absence, a medical certificate must be submitted through the HR system. The certificate must be uploaded no later than the day after it is issued.

3.2 **UK.** Employees must notify their manager by telephone before the start of their shift on the first day of absence. Self-certification for up to seven calendar days is done in the HR system. Absences longer than seven calendar days require a fit note, uploaded to the HR system.

3.3 **US.** Employees must notify their manager by telephone or the chat channel #greenville before the start of their shift. The company's sick leave policy follows state law; where the HR system shows a sick-leave balance, it is used for short-term absence. Longer absences are handled under the disability leave process — contact the HR Business Partner team.

3.4 **Return to work.** For absences of five or more working days, the manager holds a return-to-work conversation and records it in the HR system. For absences of 20 or more working days, the manager refers the employee to the occupational health service (see `ops-docs/occupational-health/oh-service.md`), which may arrange a review.

3.5 **Long-term absence.** Where an employee is absent for more than six weeks, the HR Business Partner for the site coordinates with the employee, the manager and the occupational health service. Pay during long-term absence follows the local statutory scheme and the employee's contract; the payroll calendar (`finance-docs/payroll/payroll-calendar.md`) shows the cut-offs for reporting.

## 4. Parental and carers leave

4.1 **Parental leave (Germany).** Employees may take parental leave under the statutory scheme. Requests must be submitted in writing to People & HR at least seven weeks before the intended start for the first two years, and thirteen weeks before for the third year. The HR system records the leave and the payroll team applies the parental allowance top-up where eligible.

4.2 **Shared parental leave (UK).** Eligible employees may take shared parental leave. The HR Business Partner team provides the application pack; requests must be submitted at least eight weeks before the intended start. The payroll team applies statutory pay through the payroll run.

4.3 **Family and medical leave (US).** Eligible employees may take unpaid, job-protected leave under federal and state law. Requests go through the HR Business Partner team, who coordinate with the payroll team and the HR system.

4.4 **Carers leave.** All employees may take unpaid leave to care for a dependant in an emergency. The first two working days in any rolling four-week period are paid for Germany and UK employees; US employees use their PTO balance. Longer arrangements are agreed with the manager and recorded in the HR system under "carers leave".

4.5 **Adoption and surrogacy.** The same provisions as parental leave apply, with the same notice periods. Contact the HR Business Partner team for the specific forms.

---

## 5. Other leave

5.1 **Unpaid leave.** Managers may approve unpaid leave for personal reasons, recorded in the HR system as such. Unpaid leave does not accrue holiday.

5.2 **Jury service and public duties.** Employees must inform their manager as soon as the summons or notice is received. Pay during jury service follows local law; the payroll team applies the statutory arrangements.

5.3 **Bereavement leave.** For the death of a close family member, employees receive five working days of paid leave. Additional days may be agreed with the manager and charged to holiday or unpaid leave.

---

## 6. Records and reporting

6.1 The HR system is the single record for all leave and absence. Managers must ensure that leave requests, sickness notifications and return-to-work notes are entered or approved in the system on the working day they occur.

6.2 People & HR runs a monthly review of absence patterns and reports trends to the site management teams. Where a pattern indicates a health or wellbeing issue, HR talks to the manager about a referral to the occupational health service.

6.3 Employees can see their holiday balance, accrual and history in the HR system at any time. Discrepancies should be raised with the HR Business Partner team within ten working days of the month-end close.
