# New claims and disputes

**Owner:** Legal (Americas)  
**Last edited by:** Amaka Eze (Senior Counsel, Litigation)

---

1. **Tell Legal first, not last.** If a customer claim, demand letter, lawsuit, arbitration notice, or threat of one arrives at any site — whether from a packaging line builder in Baden-Württemberg or a semiconductor-equipment customer in Greenville — do not reply, do not promise anything, and do not route it to Sales or Service. Send it to the Legal (Americas) team inbox and flag it for Amaka Eze. If it arrived by post, scan the envelope and its contents; if it was served in person, note who handed it over and where.

2. **Check for existing matters.** Before opening anything new, have someone on the team search the matter list in the litigation tracker for the customer name, the product line (MC, DU, TX, encoders), and the site. A claim often grows out of an existing RMA, an NCR, or a warranty dispute we already track. If there is an open matter, append the new document to that matter's folder and note the new claim in the matter timeline.

3. **Open the matter number.** If nothing exists, ask Amaka to create a new matter. The number comes from the Legal matter register, not from the ERP or the ticket system. The format is `K-YY-NNN` where YY is the year and NNN is the sequence. Do not invent your own. Once the number is assigned, it goes into the matter folder name and into every subsequent document about that dispute.

4. **Set up the matter folder.** Use the legal document library. Create `Matters/K-YY-NNN/` and inside it at least these subfolders:
   - `01-Correspondence` — everything sent or received, including email PDFs
   - `02-Pleadings` — complaints, answers, motions, court orders
   - `03-Evidence` — contracts, drawings, test reports, calibration records, RMA files
   - `04-Internal` — notes, strategy, timelines
   
   Upload the incoming claim or demand letter into `01-Correspondence` as the first file. Name files as `YYYY-MM-DD_description.pdf` (or `.msg` for emails). If the original came as a {{WORD_PROCESSOR}} document or an image, keep the original and also add a PDF of the printed version.

5. **Record the key dates.** In the matter register, enter the claim date, the response deadline if one is stated, the party on the other side, and the Kessendorf site or business unit involved. If the document is a lawsuit with a summons, the response deadline is usually short — put it into the Legal calendar immediately and tell the case team in the matter channel.

6. **Who talks to the other side.** From this point, only the assigned lawyer or a named paralegal communicates with the claimant, their counsel, or any regulator. The salesperson, the service engineer, the plant manager — none of them reply to the other side, even to "just acknowledge" the letter. If the other side phones a site, the person who takes the call should say they will pass the message to Legal and then actually do that the same working day. No substantive conversation.

7. **Tell the business what is happening.** For a new matter, the assigned lawyer sends a short note to the relevant site leadership and to the business unit head (for example, the Key Account Director for a logistics-equipment customer, or the Head of Hardware Engineering if the claim concerns a DU drive). The note says what arrived, what number was opened, what the next step is, and that all contact goes through Legal. Nothing more.

8. **Involve outside counsel when needed.** We work with the panel firms listed in the outside-counsel directory. Do not call a firm that is not on the panel without asking first. For a demand letter that may settle, the assigned lawyer usually handles it in-house. For a filed lawsuit, an arbitration demand, or a claim above the threshold in the outside-counsel guidance, the assigned lawyer prepares a short intake memo and sends it to the panel firm with the matter number, the key documents, and the deadline. The panel firm's first task is to confirm they have no conflict and to give us a view on the deadline.

9. **Keep the folder current.** Every letter, email, note of a call, or internal analysis goes into the matter folder. If you receive something on paper, scan it in the same working day. If you take a call about the matter, write a short note with the date, who said what, and file it in `04-Internal`. The folder is the record; if it is not in the folder, it did not happen.

10. **Close-out.** When the matter settles, is dismissed, or the limitation period passes, the assigned lawyer marks it closed in the register and adds a closing note. Paper files go to the records centre (see `legal-docs/records/records-centre.md`).

---

**If it goes wrong**

- A customer threatens suit and the site manager already promised a free replacement drive on a call. Do not try to unsay it. Log the call note, tell the assigned lawyer the same day, and let them decide how to handle it. The promise is on the record either way.
- A demand letter arrives with a deadline that has already passed. Do not panic and do not respond. Send it to Legal immediately with a note of the postmark or the email timestamp. Deadlines can often be reinstated, but only if we do not make it worse by replying late and defensively.
- Someone at a site replied to the claimant before Legal saw the letter. Get the reply, put it in the folder, and tell the assigned lawyer. We handle it; we do not hide it.
- The other side calls the UK plant asking for "the person who deals with the TX transducer warranty". The plant should take a name and number, say Legal will call back, and log it in the matter channel. No one at the plant should answer the question.
