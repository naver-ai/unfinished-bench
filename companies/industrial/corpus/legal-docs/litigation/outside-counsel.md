# Working with outside counsel

**Owner:** Legal (Americas) · **Last edited by:** Amaka Eze, Senior Counsel, Litigation

## Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Kessendorf engages, instructs and manages outside counsel across the group. It applies to all Legal team members and to any employee who commissions external legal advice. The policy covers panel firms, engagement letters, billing and e-billing, matter budgets and status reporting.

## 1. Panel firms

1.1 The Legal team maintains a panel of approved firms for each jurisdiction in which Kessendorf operates. The current panel is published on the Legal page of the intranet.

1.2 For Germany, the panel comprises firms in Reutlingen and Stuttgart. For the UK, panel firms are in the Midlands and London. For the US, panel firms are in South Carolina and Georgia. Work that touches more than one jurisdiction should be led by the firm best placed to coordinate, normally the firm in the jurisdiction where the matter is centred.

1.3 A firm outside the panel may be engaged only where the matter requires specialist expertise not available on the panel, or where a conflict prevents panel firms from acting. The engaging lawyer must note the reason on the matter record at the time of instruction.

1.4 Group companies may not engage outside counsel on a Kessendorf matter outside the panel without the prior approval of the Group General Counsel or, for Americas matters, the General Counsel, Americas.

## 2. Engagement letters

2.1 Every instruction to outside counsel must be confirmed in writing by an engagement letter before substantive work begins. The engagement letter must be issued by the Legal team member responsible for the matter.

2.2 The engagement letter must set out: the scope of work, the applicable panel rates or agreed fee structure, the billing cadence, the matter reference, the responsible Kessendorf contact, and the governing law and venue. For US matters, the engagement letter must also address privilege and the client identified for billing purposes.

2.3 Engagement letters for matters above the local office's approval threshold require countersignature by the Group General Counsel. The threshold is published in the Legal team's standing instructions.

2.4 Where a firm is engaged for a series of related matters (for example, repeated debt collection for the Telford plant or recurring employment advice for the Greenville plant), a single framework engagement letter may be used, but each new matter must still be opened separately.

## 3. Billing guidelines and e-billing

3.1 All panel firms must submit invoices through the e-billing portal. Invoices submitted by email or post will be returned to the firm.

3.2 The following are not payable without prior written approval from the responsible Legal team member:
- travel time in excess of one hour per journey
- first-class travel
- courier charges where email or a document portal would suffice
- research time that duplicates work already billed on the same matter
- charges for internal firm meetings (including "case conferences" between partners at the same firm)
- photocopying above a nominal volume, and any colour printing
- secretarial or administrative time

3.3 Firms must invoice monthly, no later than 30 days after the end of the month in which the work was performed. Invoices must show the matter reference and a narrative description of the work sufficient to assess it against the budget.

3.4 Disputed invoice lines are raised through the e-billing portal's dispute function. The firm has 30 days to respond. Any line not resolved within that period is referred to the responsible Legal team member.

3.5 Disbursements (court fees, agent fees, translation costs) must be itemised with supporting receipts. The e-billing portal requires itemisation before an invoice can be submitted.

## 4. Budgets per matter

4.1 Every litigated matter and every material transaction must have a budget agreed with the firm before the first substantive step. The budget must be recorded against the matter in the matter register.

4.2 Budgets are set by phase (investigation, pleadings, discovery, motion practice, trial, appeal, settlement) and must show assumptions about the number of witnesses, document volumes and court or tribunal stages.

4.3 The responsible Legal team member reviews the budget against actuals at each invoice cycle. A firm must notify the responsible Legal team member before exceeding any phase budget by more than 10%. Continued work without such notice may result in the excess being disputed.

4.4 For matters in the US, the budget must also be shared with the Finance team for accrual purposes. The Finance team will ask for updated forecasts at each month-end close; the responsible Legal team member should provide these within two working days of the request.

## 5. Status reports

5.1 For active litigation, the responsible Legal team member provides a status update to the Legal (Americas) team at least monthly, or more frequently where a hearing or filing deadline is approaching.

5.2 Status updates cover: key developments since the last report, next steps and deadlines, budget position against forecast, and any risks requiring management attention.

5.3 For matters involving the Telford plant or the UK drives business, the status update is copied to the Head of Legal, UK. For matters involving the Greenville plant or Americas service operations, the update is copied to the General Counsel, Americas.

5.4 Quarterly, the Legal team consolidates status reports into a summary for the Group General Counsel covering all significant matters across the group. This summary is shared with the Finance team for the quarterly risk review.

## 6. Records

6.1 All engagement letters, budgets, status reports and correspondence with outside counsel must be filed on the matter record under the matter reference. Email alone is not sufficient.

6.2 When a matter closes, the responsible Legal team member confirms on the matter record that the final invoice has been received and that the file is complete for transfer to the records centre.
