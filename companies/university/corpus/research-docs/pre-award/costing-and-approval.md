# Costing a bid and getting it approved

**Owner:** Research Development (pre-award)  
**Last edited by:** Tariq Lambert (Research Development Officer)

This page covers how bids are costed on full economic cost (fEC) in the research grants system and the approval route every bid must go through before it leaves Aldercombe. It is aimed at academics and school administrators who are putting a bid together, and at research support staff who need to know where things can get stuck.

If you are new to costing, read the funder notes in `research-docs/pre-award/funders.md` first for the quirks of each council. The rest of this page assumes you know roughly who your funder is and what they will and will not pay for.

---

## Costing on fEC

All bids are costed at 100% fEC in the research grants system, regardless of what the funder will actually pay. The system calculates the fEC from the details you enter; you do not enter a total and work backwards. The main cost categories are:

| Category | What goes in it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Directly incurred (DI) | Staff paid from the grant, consumables, equipment, travel, fieldwork, participant costs, subcontracted work | Costs must be actual, auditable and attributable to the project. Staff on fractional contracts must have their time recorded on timesheets. |
| Directly allocated (DA) | Investigator time, Estates and Indirect Costs (E&IC), any shared resource you use | Investigator time is entered as a percentage of the post, not as a sum of money. E&IC is applied automatically by the system. |
| Indirect costs | Central and departmental overheads | Applied automatically by the system at the institutional rate. Do not add your own overheads on top. |
| Exceptions | Items the funder does not normally allow (e.g. a laptop as a direct cost) | Only where the funder's guidance explicitly allows them. If in doubt, ask Research Development (pre-award) before you spend time justifying it. |

The system will flag anything entered in the wrong category. If you are not sure whether something is DI or DA, err on the side of DI but check with Hana Alvarez in Research Development (pre-award) — it changes how much of the cost the funder will meet.

### Funding at 80%

Research councils fund 80% of fEC. The other 20% is met by the university. This means the "cost to funder" on the proposal is not the same as the fEC total — the system shows both. When you are writing your justification of resources, make sure you describe the fEC cost, not the cost to funder. It is a common mistake, and it is the one reviewers notice first.

Charities and industry funders vary. Some pay 100% of fEC, some pay a fixed percentage, some pay nothing towards indirect costs. The funder notes page covers the main ones; for anything else, check the funder's own guidance before you commit to a costing.

## The approval route

Every bid must be approved before it is submitted. The route is the same whether the bid is for £5,000 or £5 million.

**Step 1 — Head of school.** The bid must be costed in the research grants system and a draft proposal attached. The head of school reviews the costing, checks the project is feasible within the school's capacity, and approves it in the system. If the head of school is a co-investigator on the bid, the dean of faculty approves instead.

**Step 2 — Research & Innovation Services (RIS).** Once the head of school has approved, the bid goes to RIS. RIS checks the costing against the funder's rules, checks the proposal narrative against the funder's call, and confirms that any match funding or institutional commitment has been agreed. Paul Brannigan's team does this; you will normally hear from one of the Research Development Officers rather than Paul himself.

**Step 3 — Submission.** After RIS approval, the bid can be submitted. The research grants system sends the final version to the funder's portal. Do not submit manually through the portal — the system must hold the final version for audit purposes.

| Lead time | What RIS needs |
|---|---|
| 5 working days | A completed costing in the research grants system, a near-final narrative, and any letters of support |
| 3 working days | Changes to a costing or narrative that has already been through RIS once |
| No lead time | Anything that goes to RIS with less than 3 working days before the funder deadline will be returned to the school. RIS cannot approve bids on the day of the deadline. |

The lead times are there because RIS checks every costing line against the funder's terms, and that takes time. If you have a funder deadline that the university cannot meet, tell Research Development (pre-award) as soon as you know — Bruno Keller can sometimes negotiate an extension with the funder, but only if he has time to do it properly.

## What happens after approval

Once RIS approves, the bid is locked in the research grants system. You can still make minor changes to the narrative, but any change to the costing means going back to the head of school. If the funder asks for a revised budget after submission, the change goes through RIS again using the 3-working-day route.

If the bid is successful, the costing transfers into the post-award side of the system and becomes the starting point for the award setup. That process is covered in `research-docs/post-award/award-setup.md`. If you are the principal investigator, make sure the costing you submitted is the one you can live with — it will be the baseline for every financial report you send the funder.

## Common reasons bids come back

- The costing includes VAT at the wrong rate. Check `finance-docs/tax/vat-notes.md` if the bid involves construction, equipment over a certain value, or anything that is not a straightforward research service.
- Investigator time exceeds the funder's cap (usually 1.0 FTE per project, but some funders are stricter).
- The justification of resources describes a different amount to the costing.
- Travel is costed at standard economy rather than the funder's rate.
- The head of school has not actually approved the bid in the system — a verbal "that's fine" does not count.

If you are not sure whether something will be an issue, ask Research Development (pre-award) before you put the bid in front of the head of school. It saves a round trip.
