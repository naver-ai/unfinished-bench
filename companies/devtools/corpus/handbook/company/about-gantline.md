# About Gantline

Gantline Software, Lda. builds developer tools from Lisbon, where the company was founded. What became the `gnt` command-line tool started as an open-source side project by a small group of engineers who wanted a faster way to build and ship code without the ceremony that came with the toolchains of the time. The project found traction quickly, and the company formed around it a few years later to provide the hosted platform that people kept asking for.

Today we sell two things. The first is `gnt` itself: an open-source CLI for building, testing and shipping code, which anyone can download and use freely. The second is Gantline Cloud, our hosted development platform. Gantline Cloud brings together code hosting, hosted CI runners, cloud workspaces, code search, a public API, a web and desktop editor, and mobile apps. Teams buy it on per-seat plans, and larger customers tend to land on annual contracts through the sales team. Most of our revenue comes from Gantline Cloud, but the CLI remains the front door for a lot of users — it is how people first meet us, and a meaningful share of Cloud signups start with someone installing `gnt` for a side project and then bringing it into work.

Gantline Cloud launched a few years after the company was founded, and for a while the whole business ran out of the Lisbon office. The Portland office opened later as we started selling seriously to US customers. It is home to most of our go-to-market teams — sales, customer success, and some of the growth functions — though engineering remains largely in Lisbon. About 400 people work at Gantline across the two offices, with a handful of fully remote roles. US customers are served by our US subsidiary, Gantline Inc., so contracts and invoicing for that side of the business flow through the Portland entity.

Our customers are engineering teams more than individual developers. The sweet spot is a team of maybe a dozen to a few hundred engineers who want the convenience of a hosted platform without giving up the speed of a local toolchain. We also have a small number of large customers who run Gantline self-managed inside their own infrastructure; those installs are few and are handled directly by the teams that own the platform, not through a general self-hosted product line. On the other end, we see a long tail of individual developers and small teams using the free tier of Gantline Cloud, which gives them a way to grow into paid seats as their needs change.

The open-source community around `gnt` is a real part of how the company operates, not just a marketing story. The CLI is developed in the open, and community contributions land in it regularly. Some of the people who started as contributors now work here; others remain active maintainers outside the company. The developer relations team looks after the community forums and the docs site, and the content team publishes engineering posts that often come out of work done in the open. Community input also shapes the roadmap — a surprising number of product decisions trace back to an issue on the gnt tracker filed by someone we had never met.
