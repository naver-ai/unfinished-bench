## Verifying the caller

**Q: The caller says they're the member but I can't find them in the member portal.**
A: Ask for their member ID from their physical or digital ID card first. If they don't have it, ask for full name, date of birth, and current address. Try searching by name and date of birth in the member portal before asking for anything else. If the address doesn't match what we have, ask if they've moved recently and update it after they verify a piece of information from the file (like the last claim they had or the primary care provider listed).

**Q: A spouse calls asking about the member's claims. What can I share?**
A: If the spouse is on the same policy, they're fine to discuss the member's benefits and claims. If they're not on the policy, take a message and have the member call back, or offer to do a three-way call with the member on the line.

**Q: An employer group contact calls wanting a status on an employee's claim.**
A: We don't discuss individual member claims with employer group contacts unless there's a signed authorization on file from the member. Check the member's record for an active authorization before sharing anything. If there isn't one, tell the employer contact we can't discuss the claim and point them to the member to call us.

## ID cards

**Q: The member's ID card looks different from what I'm used to seeing.**
A: The {{MANAGED_PLAN_PROGRAM}} cards were redesigned this year — they now have the member's name on the front in larger type and the member ID is still the same format (KH plus nine digits). The employer group cards haven't changed. If the card doesn't look like either format, ask the member to read off the numbers and check against what we have in the member portal.

**Q: How do I send a replacement ID card?**
A: Use the "Print ID card" button in the member portal. The PDF that generates is the same one the member can download from the patient app. The card can be mailed to the member's address on file or sent to the secure message center on the patient app — ask which they prefer.

**Q: A provider says the member's ID card is expired.**
A: Our ID cards don't have expiration dates printed on them. If a provider is seeing an expiration date, it's likely from another insurer's card that got mixed in. Ask the provider to read the member ID and verify through the provider portal.

## Eligibility questions

**Q: The caller wants to know if a specific service is covered.**
A: Check the member's benefit summary in the member portal first. If the service is listed as "covered subject to medical necessity" or isn't listed, that's when we route to utilization management for a pre-service benefit review. Don't guess — if you're not sure whether something is covered, tell the member a benefits specialist will call them back, and send it to the utilization management queue.

**Q: The caller's coverage ended last month but they're asking about a claim from before.**
A: If the date of service falls within their active coverage period, the claim is still processed as normal. If the date of service falls after their termination date, tell them the claim will be denied as not covered and they'll get an EOB explaining it.

**Q: A member on a group plan says their employer told them coverage is active but our system shows a termination date.**
A: Ask the member to confirm their last day worked. Sometimes the employer reports a retroactive termination that matches the actual separation date. If the member disputes it, escalate to the group enrollment team through the member portal's escalation tab — don't tell the member their employer is wrong.

## Explaining an EOB

**Q: The caller doesn't understand why their claim was paid at a lower amount than the provider billed.**
A: Walk through the EOB line by line. Explain the allowed amount, what the plan paid, and what the member owes (if anything). Use the exact language from the EOB. If they're asking why the allowed amount is lower than the billed amount, explain the network contract rate.

**Q: The caller says the EOB shows a service they didn't receive.**
A: Don't speculate. Ask the caller to verify the date of service and the provider name on the EOB. If they still say they didn't receive the service, tell them we'll open a claim inquiry. Create the inquiry in the claims adjudication system with the reason code for "member disputes service" and give them the inquiry reference number.

**Q: The member asks why their EOB says "not covered" when their doctor said it was covered.**
A: Explain that the EOB reflects what the plan covers based on the member's benefits at the time of service, and that the provider's office doesn't always have the full benefit details. If they want a formal review, they can file an appeal — see below for that process.

## Balance billing on out-of-network care

**Q: The caller went to an out-of-network ER and the hospital is billing them for the full amount.**
A: Explain that out-of-network providers aren't contracted with us, so they can bill the member for amounts beyond what our plan pays. If it was an emergency, our plan covers out-of-network emergency care at the in-network level — but the provider can still balance bill. Tell them we can send a letter to the provider explaining our payment, but we can't force the provider to accept it. Offer to mail the letter and note the member's account.

**Q: The caller is being billed by an out-of-network lab that their in-network doctor used.**
A: This depends on the plan. Some of our group plans have the "no surprise billing" protection for labs ordered by in-network providers — check the member's benefit summary. If it's a {{MANAGED_PLAN_PROGRAM}} plan, the No Surprises Act rules apply. If neither applies, walk through the same explanation as above.

## Taking an appeal or grievance

**Q: The caller wants to appeal a denial.**
A: Take the appeal verbally or offer to mail the appeal form. Gather the member ID, the claim or service in question, the date of the denial letter, and the reason they're appealing. Create the appeal in the appeals system (the same one used for grievances) and give them the appeal reference number. Tell them they'll get a written decision within the timeframe required for their line of business — standard appeals are decided within thirty days for group and individual plans, and we prioritize expedited appeals when a delay could seriously jeopardize their health.

**Q: The caller is upset about how a claim was handled and wants to file a complaint.**
A: A grievance is fine to take over the phone. Log it in the appeals system with the grievance reason, the date of the experience, and what happened. Let them know the grievance will be reviewed and they'll get a written response. Don't try to resolve the grievance on the call — just acknowledge their concern and make sure it gets logged.

**Q: Can a caller file an appeal on behalf of a family member?**
A: If they're the member's legal representative (parent of a minor, legal guardian, or have a power of attorney), yes. Ask for verification of the relationship. Otherwise, take the appeal from the member directly.

## Warm transfers to utilization management

**Q: The caller asks if a service needs prior authorization.**
A: Check the prior authorization list in the member portal. If it's not on the list, tell them they can proceed with the service without prior authorization. If it is on the list, transfer the caller to utilization management — but first let the caller know why you're transferring and what information to have ready (the service, the provider, and the date if scheduled).

**Q: The caller was told by their provider that we denied a prior authorization request.**
A: Transfer to utilization management. Before you do, tell the caller they may want to have the provider's office on the line if they're calling from there, and let them know the utilization management team may need to discuss the clinical details with the provider directly.

**Q: The caller wants to know the status of a pending prior authorization.**
A: This is a utilization management question. Warm transfer to the utilization management queue and stay on the line until someone picks up. If no one is available, take the member's number and send a message to the utilization management team through the member portal with a note to call the member back.
