# Exam boards

**Owner:** Examinations & Assessment  
**Last edited by:** Ethan Marchetti (Assessment Officer)

## Purpose and scope

This document sets out how module boards and progression and award boards operate at Aldercombe, including membership, the order in which boards sit after each assessment period, how papers are prepared from the student record system, and how confirmed results are published to students. It applies to all taught provision on the Hollins Hill and City campuses and at Aldercombe University Malaysia, except where noted.

## 1. Types of board

1.1 Module boards consider module results for a single module or a small group of related modules. They are chaired by the module leader or, where the module leader is unavailable, by the head of the relevant school.

1.2 Progression and award boards (sometimes called "main boards") consider students' overall performance for a level or stage of study and make decisions on progression, award classification, compensation, and condonement.

1.3 Separate progression and award boards sit for each faculty, with the Faculty Progression and Award Board as the senior board for that faculty.

## 2. Membership

2.1 Module boards comprise the module leader, all teaching staff for the module, and at least one member of the relevant school's assessment lead team. The Examinations & Assessment team provides a secretary.

2.2 Progression and award boards comprise:
- the chair (usually the Deputy Vice-Chancellor or a Dean of Faculty appointed by the Academic Registrar);
- the heads of school (or their nominees) for the faculty;
- the faculty's Directors of Studies;
- at least one external examiner for each programme under consideration;
- the Academic Registrar or their nominee (the Deputy Academic Registrar (Assessment) normally attends);
- a representative from Examinations & Assessment;
- a student representative from the Students' Union, where available.

2.3 External examiners are entitled to attend the boards for the programmes they examine, and are invited to comment on borderline cases.

2.4 No member of staff may sit on a board that considers their own registered study or that of a close relative. The chair must be told of any such interest before the board begins.

## 3. External examiners

3.1 External examiners are appointed by the Academic Registry on the recommendation of the relevant school. Appointments are for a maximum of four years, renewable once.

3.2 External examiners receive their papers no later than five working days before the board sits. They must confirm receipt of the papers and submit their reports through the external examiner portal in the student record system.

3.3 Where an external examiner is unable to attend a board, they may submit written comments in advance. The board must consider those comments and record that it has done so.

3.4 Schools must respond to external examiner reports within ten working days of the board. Responses are routed through the school's head and copied to Examinations & Assessment.

## 4. Order of boards after each assessment period

4.1 The order of boards after each assessment period is:

1. Module boards for all modules in the faculty;
2. Faculty progression and award boards;
3. Any cross-faculty boards required for joint honours programmes.

4.2 Module boards must sit before the relevant progression and award board so that any module-level changes are reflected in the main board's papers.

4.3 For the main assessment period (May–June), all module boards must have sat by the end of the allocated window set out in the academic calendar. For supplementary assessment (August), module boards sit in a compressed window and must be completed no later than two working days before the relevant progression and award board.

4.4 Where a module has no students to consider, the module board may be stood down by the head of school. The head of school must tell Examinations & Assessment so that the record is updated.

## 5. Preparing board papers

5.1 Examinations & Assessment extracts module and student result data from the student record system to produce the standard board paper pack. The pack includes:
- the agenda;
- module results sheets (for module boards);
- student progression and award lists (for progression and award boards);
- the previous board's minutes and action log;
- any interim assessment data or additional evidence submitted by schools.

5.2 Schools must submit any late or corrected marks, extenuating circumstances outcomes, and academic misconduct outcomes to Examinations & Assessment no later than three working days before the relevant module board.

5.3 The student record system applies the published assessment rules to generate provisional outcomes. Schools should check these provisional outcomes and flag any that appear incorrect to Examinations & Assessment before the board sits, rather than raising them on the day.

5.4 Papers are distributed to board members through the student record system's board portal. Members must not print or share board papers beyond the board membership. Papers contain personal data and should be handled under the information classification guidance in the staff handbook.

5.5 For boards at Aldercombe University Malaysia, papers are prepared from the same student record system instance. Examinations & Assessment at Hollins Hill produces the papers and shares them with the Kuala Lumpur campus team through the usual secure route.

## 6. Running the board

6.1 The chair is responsible for ensuring the board considers all students on the agenda and that decisions are recorded in the student record system during or immediately after the meeting.

6.2 The board secretary (from Examinations & Assessment) records decisions and any conditions attached to an outcome. Decisions are entered against the student's record using the board decision codes documented in the student record system.

6.3 Where the board reaches a different outcome from the provisional one, the chair confirms the reason and the secretary records it. The outcome must be within the scope of the regulations for the programme concerned.

6.4 Progression and award boards act on the marks before them as confirmed by the relevant module boards. Any query about a mark should be referred back to the module board process.

6.5 Minutes and the action log are circulated by the secretary within five working days of the board. Schools must complete their actions before the next board in the cycle.

## 7. Publishing confirmed results

7.1 Once the progression and award board has confirmed outcomes, Examinations & Assessment publishes results to students through the student portal. This is the only channel by which results are officially released.

7.2 Results are published on the dates set out in the academic calendar. For the main assessment period, this is the first working day after all faculty boards have sat. For supplementary assessment, results are published within three working days of the final faculty board.

7.3 Students at Aldercombe University Malaysia receive their results through the same student portal. Local staff do not release results directly.

7.4 Where a student has an outstanding debt to the university, the student record system applies the normal holds on transcript and certificate release. This does not prevent the student from viewing their results in the student portal.

7.5 After results are published, the student record system marks the relevant assessment period as closed. Changes to published results are handled under the grade changes process in `registry-docs/exams/grade-changes.md`.
