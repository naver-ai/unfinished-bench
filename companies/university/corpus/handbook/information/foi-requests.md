# Freedom of information requests

**Owner:** Information Governance  
**Last edited by:** Tessa Kiplagat (Records Manager)

The university is a public authority under the Freedom of Information Act. This means anyone can ask us for information we hold, in writing, and we normally have to respond within twenty working days. This page covers the basics — what to do when a request lands with you, and what Information Governance will ask from you.

## What counts as a Freedom of Information request?

A request is any written request for information that:

- is made by anyone, anywhere in the world
- is in writing (email, letter, web form, social media message — if it's recorded in text, it counts)
- describes the information they want, even if they don't say "Freedom of Information" or "FOI"

If someone asks for information in person or over the phone, it is not an FOI request, but you should still note what they asked and tell them to put it in writing if they want a formal response.

## What should I do when I get one?

Forward it to Information Governance (ig@aldercombe.ac.uk) straight away, without responding to the requester yourself. If the request came by post, scan or photograph the letter and send that through; keep the original.

The twenty-working-day clock starts the day after we receive the request. If Information Governance does not have the request in hand until a few days later, that is time we lose.

## What will Information Governance ask me?

Usually a short list of questions:

- Do we hold the information? Check the shared drives, the student record system, the finance system, the estates helpdesk, and any other system that might reasonably hold it.
- Where is it? Give the file path or system and the record reference if there is one.
- Can you send it over? Email the relevant files or a summary to the Information Governance mailbox. If the files are large, put them in the shared FOI folder and tell us where they are.
- Anything sensitive in there? Flag anything that looks like personal data, commercially confidential material, or draft advice — we will decide on exemptions, but your flag helps us look in the right places.

## How long do I have to get back to Information Governance?

As soon as you can. If you think it will take more than a couple of working days to pull the information together, tell the team so they can plan. They will usually need your material by the mid-point of the twenty-working-day period at the latest.

## What exemptions come up most?

In plain terms:

- **Personal data** — we cannot release information that identifies a living individual if doing so would breach data protection principles. This is the most common one.
- **Commercial confidentiality** — for example, details of a supplier's pricing in a contract we hold. We weigh the public interest before applying this.
- **Legal professional privilege** — advice from Legal Services & Governance, or correspondence with our external solicitors.
- **Information intended for future publication** — if we are planning to publish something anyway, we may withhold it until then.
- **Vexatious or repeated requests** — the Act lets us refuse requests that are obsessive, harassing, or clearly not serious. Information Governance decides this, not the receiving team.

There are others, but these are the ones staff see regularly.

## What if I think the request is about my area but I'm not sure?

Send it to Information Governance anyway. They will route it to the right team and copy you in if they need your input. It is better to over-forward than to sit on a request wondering.

## What about requests for environmental information?

The Environmental Information Regulations cover things like emissions, noise, waste, and the condition of our buildings and grounds. The rules are broadly similar, and the same team handles them. If a request looks environmental, flag it as such when you forward it.

## What about requests about the academy?

Kestrel Vale Academy is covered by the same Act because the university sponsors it through the Aldercombe Learning Trust. If a request mentions the academy, forward it to Information Governance as usual — the team will work with the academy office.

## Anything else I should know?

- We have a disclosure log on the website. If you are asked for something we have already released, Information Governance may point the requester there rather than re-answering.
- The IT service desk portal has a "Records and information" category if you need to raise a request to retrieve archived material.
- If you are not sure whether something is a request, treat it as one and forward it. The cost of a false positive is small; the cost of a missed deadline is not.
