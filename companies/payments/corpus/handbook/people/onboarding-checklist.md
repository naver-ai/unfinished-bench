# Onboarding checklist (managers)

Owner: People team · Last edited by Aisling Moran

This checklist is for managers bringing a new person into their team. It covers the practical steps from offer acceptance through the first month. The People team sends the new joiner their own copy of the induction guide; this is the manager-side view.

## Before day one

- [ ] Confirm start date and office location (Dublin, Vilnius or Austin) with the People team at least five working days ahead.
- [ ] Submit the access request through the service desk form (see `handbook/it/access-requests.md`) — laptop, building access, and the systems listed on the team's access profile. If you are not sure which profile applies, ask Elias or Milan in People Operations.
- [ ] Request a buddy for the new joiner. Pick someone on the team who has been here at least a quarter and is not the manager. Tell the buddy what you expect: a first-week coffee, daily check-ins, and being the person for "where do I find…" questions.
- [ ] Book the new joiner into the next available learning platform cohorts for AML awareness, security basics, and data protection. All three are mandatory and should be completed within the first two weeks. The learning platform sends reminders automatically.
- [ ] Make sure the desk, laptop dock, and any second monitor are ready. For Austin, coordinate with Maribel's team; for Vilnius, the operations centre front desk handles it.
- [ ] Add the start date to the team calendar and invite the new joiner to the relevant chat channels (#general, the team channel, and the office channel for their location).
- [ ] If the role touches payments operations or treasury, confirm with the team lead that shift handover notes are ready for the first week.

## First day

- [ ] Meet the new joiner at reception (Dublin and Vilnius) or the Austin office lobby at the agreed time.
- [ ] Walk through the office — kitchen, quiet rooms, bike storage, and where the team sits.
- [ ] Make sure they can sign in to their laptop and the merchant dashboard or the back office, depending on their role. If sign-in fails, point them at #it-help rather than trying to fix it yourself.
- [ ] Introduce them to the buddy and at least three people on the team. Do not do a full-team tour on day one; it is overwhelming.
- [ ] Book a thirty-minute 1:1 for mid-afternoon to check how the first day has gone and answer questions.
- [ ] Confirm they have been added to the correct distribution lists and calendar invites (team meetings, company updates, office events).

## First two weeks

- [ ] Check that the learning platform courses are progressing. The mandatory three should be done by the end of week two; if not, agree a catch-up plan with the new joiner.
- [ ] Arrange shadowing sessions: one with the support team (even if the role is not support-facing) and one with whoever owns the data or systems the team depends on. The point is exposure to how the business actually runs, not depth.
- [ ] Review the team's working agreements and the on-call guide if the role will be on call. Do not put a new joiner on the rota until they have completed the relevant training and the team lead has signed off.
- [ ] Ensure they have read the handbook sections that matter for their role — acceptable use, data classification, and the expenses page at minimum. The People team covers this in induction, but you should confirm it happened.
- [ ] Set three to five objectives for the first quarter and share them in writing. These should be about learning the business and the systems, not delivery targets.
- [ ] Introduce them to their counterpart in the relevant function (compliance, treasury, risk, or finance) so they know who to go to with questions later.

## First month

- [ ] Arrange a session with the support team (in person if same office, otherwise a video call) covering the common merchant questions and where to escalate. Maribel's team in Austin and the Vilnius support leads both run these; book through the team channel.
- [ ] Check in on the mandatory training completion and chase via the learning platform if anything is outstanding.
- [ ] Have a first-month review conversation covering how the onboarding has felt, whether the buddy relationship is working, and any access or tooling gaps. Adjust the buddy pairing if it is not a fit.
- [ ] Confirm the new joiner has met the rest of the team informally — a team lunch or coffee works, and the People team can expense it through the usual route.
- [ ] Add a handover note to the team's onboarding folder with anything that was missing or confusing, so the next hire goes more smoothly.

The People team runs a quarterly check on onboarding completion rates. If you have any questions about the checklist itself, reach out to Ruth or Aisling in the People team.
