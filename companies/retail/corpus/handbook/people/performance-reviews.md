# Performance conversations

Twice a year, every team at Mossgrove runs a check-in cycle. These are the moments where you and your line manager step back from the day-to-day and talk about how things are going, what you want to get out of the next few months, and anything that is getting in the way of your work. They are not the same as your 1:1s — those are for operational stuff. Check-ins are about the bigger picture.

We used to have a ratings system attached to these conversations. That is gone. We dropped it two cycles ago, and we are not bringing it back. Ratings pushed people towards negotiating for a score rather than talking honestly about their work, and they made it harder for managers to have open conversations about development. Your check-in is not a grade. It is a conversation.

The People team publishes the cycle dates and the prompts for each round. The prompts are the same for every role, from the shop floor in stores to the wholesale team to the folks in Leeds, Dublin and Columbus. If you work at Mossgrove, you get the same check-in. For store colleagues, the conversation normally happens with your store manager or assistant manager, and the back office has a specific workflow for recording it — the POS back office has a link through to the check-in form.

Each cycle runs for about three weeks. Managers are expected to have the conversation and record it in the HR system before the cycle closes. If you have not had your check-in by the time the cycle closes, your manager hears from the People team. If you are a manager, you should block time in your calendar early in the cycle — it is your job to make sure the conversation happens, not the other way round.

After each cycle, the People team runs calibration. This is not about ratings — it is about making sure that the way we are talking about people is fair and consistent across teams. Calibration sessions bring together managers from a department, plus a People Partner, and they go through the notes from each check-in. The point is to catch blind spots: is one manager being much harder on their team than another? Is someone being held back because their manager does not see what they do? Is a strong performer getting overlooked because they are quiet in meetings? The output of calibration is not a score. It is a shared view of who is doing well, who needs support, and what the team should be doing differently.

If you are a manager, you will be invited to calibration for your area. It is not optional. If you cannot make the session, tell your People Partner and they will reschedule you. The sessions are confidential — what gets said in calibration stays in calibration, and the notes that come out of it are only visible to the People team and the relevant director.

If you have concerns about how your check-in went, or about how calibration treated someone on your team, the first thing to do is speak to your line manager. If that does not work, or the concern is about your manager, speak to your People Partner. The People Partner team covers every part of the business: Tomas works with the platform and data teams, Selin covers stores and wholesale, and Leon covers finance, marketing and the exec office. If you are not sure who your People Partner is, the HR system has a page that tells you.

We also run a mid-cycle pulse survey a few weeks after the check-ins close. That is anonymous, and it feeds into the People team's planning for the next cycle. It is short — it should take you about five minutes — and it is the best way we have of knowing whether the conversations are actually useful.

The check-in prompts change a little each cycle, but the shape does not. You will always be asked what has gone well, what has been hard, and what you want to focus on next. The conversation is yours as much as your manager's — bring examples, bring questions, bring things you have noticed that nobody else has. The best check-ins are the ones where the manager talks less than the person they manage.
