# HIPAA basics for staff

**Owner:** Compliance & privacy  
**Last edited by:** Ebony Santos (Privacy Analyst)

The questions below are ones we actually hear from staff. If yours isn't here, ask in #security or send a note to the Compliance & privacy team.

---

**I need to look up a patient's record to do my job. Do I need to ask anyone first?**

No. If the access is part of your assigned duties, you don't need a special request. But it has to be *minimum necessary* — open only the parts of the chart you need, not the whole record. The EHR logs every view, and Compliance & privacy runs periodic audits of access. If a review shows you opened records you didn't need for your role, that becomes a workforce privacy matter.

**Can I look up my own chart, or my child's or parent's, through the EHR?**

No. Looking up your own record, a family member's record, or a co-worker's record through your work access is a violation even if you have a legitimate reason. Use the patient portal or the patient app for your own records, and for a family member use the portal or contact the HIM department (medical records) about the appropriate release process. The same applies if a friend or neighbour asks you to "just peek" at something.

**What if I'm caring for a family member as part of my job? Like I work in the clinic they're a patient at?**

If the family member is literally on your schedule or you're the staff member assigned to their care, you still shouldn't open their record without checking with your supervisor first. When it's avoidable, the department should assign another staff member. If it isn't avoidable, your supervisor should note it, and you should only access the parts needed for that visit. Don't browse.

**I saw a co-worker looking up a record. What do I do?**

Report it. You can tell your manager, or go straight to Compliance & privacy, or file a report through the safety event reporting system — that route works for privacy concerns too, not just patient safety events. You don't need proof; a concern is enough. We follow up and you won't be expected to confront the person.

**Can I talk about a patient in the hallway or the cafeteria?**

No. Not with names, and not with enough detail that someone could figure out who it is. This includes phone calls in public areas. If you need to discuss a patient, use a private office or a closed meeting room, or the chat channel for your team if it's internal and appropriate. Remember the elevators and the stairwells at the South Bend campus are not private.

**What about the nurses' station? We discuss patients there all the time.**

The nurses' station is a work area, but that doesn't mean you can talk freely. Keep voices low, don't call out diagnoses or test results across the room, and don't leave patient information on a monitor where visitors or other patients can see it. If you're discussing something sensitive, take it to a private room or use the EHR's secure messaging instead.

**I need to fax something to an outside provider. What are the rules?**

Use the fax machines in the records office or the designated fax areas on each floor — not the one in the open admin area. Confirm the fax number before sending, and use a cover sheet marked confidential. For the Kalamazoo research institute, study-related faxes go through the same process; the fax logs in the research office are checked regularly.

**Can I email patient information?**

Only through the secure email function in the EHR or the patient portal. Regular email (including your @kellsbrookhealth.org mailbox) is not approved for patient information. If you've received patient information in regular email, don't forward it — ask the sender to use the secure route. For study data, follow the data-sharing guidance in research-docs/data/data-sharing.md.

**What about texting or chat? Can I send patient information in chat?**

No. The company chat is not approved for patient information. If you need to share clinical details in writing, use the EHR's messaging. The only exception is if a patient's care truly can't wait and you're sending to another Kellsbrook staff member who is actively caring for that patient — and even then, it should be the minimum necessary and you should note it in the chart.

**I lost my badge. What do I do?**

Report it immediately to the service desk through tickets, and let your manager know. A lost badge on its own isn't a HIPAA issue, but a lost badge combined with an unlocked workstation is. If you lost your phone or laptop, report that to the service desk right away — they can remote-wipe it. Don't wait to see if it turns up.

**I left my workstation unlocked and someone used it. What happens?**

That's a privacy incident. Report it to your manager and to Compliance & privacy. The EHR audit log will show what was accessed from your session. The more quickly you report, the better we can assess whether anything was accessed that shouldn't have been. Leaving a workstation unlocked is a violation of the acceptable use policy, but reporting it promptly matters more than the initial mistake.

**I work remotely sometimes. Is that okay with patient information?**

Yes, as long as you're on the VPN and using a Kellsbrook-issued device. Don't access the EHR or other clinical systems from a personal device. If you're working from home, make sure no one else can see your screen. If you're in a coffee shop or other public place, you shouldn't be working with patient information at all — wait until you're somewhere private. See handbook/it/devices.md and it-docs/network/remote-access-vpn.md for the details.

**A patient asked me to print their records so they can take them to another doctor. Can I do that?**

Yes, but use the release of information process — the patient can sign the form and HIM (medical records) will handle it, or you can direct them to the patient portal where they can download their records themselves. Don't print records just because a patient asks you to; the portal is faster and the record will be complete. If a patient needs records urgently for an appointment the same day, contact the records office and they'll tell you what can be done.

**A patient's family member called and wants test results over the phone. Can I give them out?**

Only if the patient has a current release on file, and only to the people named on that release. If there's no release, tell the caller to ask the patient to add them through the portal or contact HIM. Even with a release, use the patient portal or a secure message if possible — phone calls aren't always private, and you can't be sure who's on the line.

**What counts as a privacy incident? I'm not sure if what I saw is reportable.**

If you're not sure, report it. Privacy incidents include: accessing a record you didn't need, sharing information with someone not authorised, losing a device or badge that could lead to access, faxing or emailing to the wrong place, leaving patient information visible, or talking about a patient where others could hear. You can report through the safety event reporting system, or directly to Compliance & privacy. You won't get in trouble for reporting a good-faith concern.

**What happens after I report?**

Compliance & privacy triages it. Depending on what happened, we may look at the EHR audit log, talk to the people involved, and decide whether it needs to be treated as a breach. Curtis Ambrose's team handles any notification obligations. If it's a workforce matter (like someone accessing records without a work reason), it goes through HR. You'll get confirmation that we received the report, but we generally can't tell you the outcome.

**I work in the Cork office. Do the same rules apply?**

Yes. The Cork medical centre handles the same kinds of patient information, and Irish data protection law applies there. The expectations are the same: minimum necessary, no looking up your own or family records, no talking about patients in public areas, secure fax and email only. If you have questions specific to Cork, ask in #cork-office or contact Compliance & privacy. The research institute's European trials office follows the same rules for study participant information.
