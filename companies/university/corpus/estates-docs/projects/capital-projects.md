# How capital projects run

Capital Projects sits within Estates & Facilities and manages all non-routine construction and refurbishment work across the Aldercombe estate — Hollins Hill, City campus, the Kuala Lumpur branch, and the Kestrel Vale Academy site. If it costs more than the delegated maintenance budget and changes the fabric of a building, it comes through us.

## Business case

Every project starts with a business case. The sponsor (usually the faculty or professional service that needs the space) works with a Project Manager from our team to set out the scope, the benefits, the rough cost range, and the options considered. The case goes to the Capital Projects Board, which meets monthly and is chaired by the Director of Estates & Facilities. The Board decides whether to proceed to the design stages, whether to send the case back for more work, or whether to stop it altogether. Projects above the Board's delegated limit go to the University Executive Board for approval.

## Project board

Once a project is approved, a project board is set up. The board includes the sponsor, the Project Manager, a representative from Finance, and — for anything touching teaching space — someone from the timetabling team. The board meets at key decision points, not on a fixed cycle. Its job is to approve the budget at each stage, sign off the brief, and resolve anything the project team cannot. Day-to-day decisions stay with the Project Manager.

## Design stages

We use the standard {{ARCHITECTS_BODY|code}} stages. The Project Manager appoints an architect and specialist consultants through a competitive process, usually from the university's framework of approved practices. Stage 2 (concept design) is where the brief gets tested against the budget. Stage 3 (developed design) is where the detail is fixed — this is the point at which the room booking system and the estates helpdesk get consulted about furniture, access, and how the space will be managed once built. Stage 4 (technical design) produces the drawings and specifications that go out to tender.

## Procurement of contractors

Contractors are appointed by tender on the basis of the Stage 4 package. For larger projects we run a two-stage tender; for smaller ones a single stage. Tenders are evaluated on price, quality, and capacity to deliver alongside a live campus. References are always taken up. The contract used is the current JCT standard form. The Project Manager is the point of contact for the contractor throughout; contractors do not approach other university teams directly about variations or payments.

## Handover to facilities management

Handover is a formal process, not a date on a programme. The contractor must provide O&M manuals, as-built drawings, and a full set of commissioning records before the building is accepted. The estates helpdesk sets up the asset records and planned maintenance schedules in the helpdesk system at this point. The Project Manager walks the building with the Estates & Facilities operations team and the end users to agree the snagging list. Practical completion is certified only when the snagging list is agreed, and the building is then available for use.

## Defects period

After practical completion, the contractor remains responsible for defects for a twelve-month period. The project team does a review visit at six months and another just before the end of the period. Any defects found are logged through the estates helpdesk and chased with the contractor. At the end of the period, the Project Manager certifies that the defects have been made good and the final retention is released.

## Lessons learned

Every project above a set value closes with a lessons learned review. The Project Manager circulates the notes to the team and to the Capital Projects Board. The notes are kept in the project folder on the estates drive so that the same mistakes are not repeated on the next project. Common themes in recent reviews have been the need to involve the helpdesk earlier, the importance of getting the sponsor to sign off the brief in writing, and the value of a proper traffic management plan during works on the Hollins Hill campus.
