# About Aldercombe

Aldercombe University began as the Hollins Hill Teacher Training College in the late nineteenth century, offering residential courses for schoolteachers in the south-west. It became a college of education, then a polytechnic in the 1970s, and was granted university status in the early 1990s. The name Aldercombe was chosen for the surrounding valley; the original college building is still in use on the Hollins Hill campus as the administrative heart of the university.

Today the university is home to around 16,000 students across three faculties. The Faculty of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences is the largest by student numbers, followed by the Faculty of Science and Engineering and the Faculty of Health and Social Care. Each faculty has its own academic schools and research centres, and all three are represented on the University Executive Board by their deans.

The main campus at Hollins Hill sits on the eastern edge of the city, with the original Victorian buildings at its core and newer teaching spaces, laboratories and student accommodation radiating out from there. Most of the university's professional services teams are based at Hollins Hill, including Finance, Registry, Research and Innovation Services, and IT. The City campus, about a mile from the centre, houses parts of the Faculty of Health and Social Care, the business school, and a number of postgraduate teaching rooms. The two campuses are linked by a frequent shuttle bus during term time.

Aldercombe University Malaysia operates from a purpose-built campus in Kuala Lumpur. The branch campus offers undergraduate programmes in business, computing and engineering, validated by the university and taught by locally appointed academic staff under the supervision of the relevant faculties. Around 2,000 students are enrolled there. The Kuala Lumpur campus has its own administrative team, led by the Chief Executive and Provost of the Malaysia campus, who reports to the Deputy Vice-Chancellor.

Aldercombe Estates Services Ltd is the university's wholly-owned facilities management subsidiary. It employs roughly 300 staff across cleaning, catering, security, portering, grounds and maintenance, and provides services to the Hollins Hill and City campuses, the Kuala Lumpur campus, and a small number of commercial properties the university owns in the city centre. The subsidiary is managed through the Estates directorate and reports into the Chief Operating Officer.

The Development & Alumni Relations office is its own directorate, working closely with Communications & Marketing, and manages the university's fundraising, alumni engagement and supporter stewardship. The office runs the university's appeals, cultivates major donors, and maintains the supporter CRM used for all giving and contact history. Fundraising income is processed by the Income team in Finance, with Gift Aid claimed through the same processes described in the finance documentation.

Aldercombe Learning Trust, the university-sponsored multi-academy trust, operates Kestrel Vale Academy, a primary school on the western edge of the city. The school has around 400 pupils and is governed by a local governing body with university representation. The trust's central functions are hosted within the university's HR and Finance directorates, with day-to-day school operations led by the headteacher and the academy's own administrative staff.

Staff numbers are broadly split as follows: around 2,000 academic and academic-related staff, with the largest concentrations in the Faculty of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences and the Faculty of Science and Engineering; approximately 1,200 professional services staff across the university's directorates, the majority based at Hollins Hill; around 300 staff employed by Aldercombe Estates Services; and about 120 staff at the Kuala Lumpur campus. The Development & Alumni Relations office has a team of 25, and Kestrel Vale Academy employs around 50 teaching and support staff. These figures fluctuate during the academic year as teaching contracts and research posts change.

The University Executive Board, chaired by the Vice-Chancellor, sets institutional strategy and oversees the budget. The Council is the governing body, with Senate responsible for academic governance. A list of current board members and senior postholders is maintained on the governance pages of the staff intranet.
