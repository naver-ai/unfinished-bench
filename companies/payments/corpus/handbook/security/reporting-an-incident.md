# Reporting a security incident

**Owner:** Security operations (SOC)  
**Last edited by:** Beatriz Fonseca (Detection Engineer)

## What counts as an incident

Report anything that might involve unauthorised access, loss of data, or a breach of our controls. Examples include:

- A lost or stolen laptop, phone, or hardware token
- Clicking a link or opening an attachment in a suspicious email
- Entering credentials into a page that looked wrong
- Sending merchant data to the wrong recipient (wrong email address, wrong API endpoint, wrong partner)
- A merchant or partner reporting activity they did not recognise
- Found something in a system log that you cannot explain

When in doubt, report it. The SOC would rather close a false alarm than hear about something late.

## How to report

1. Go to the `#security` channel in chat and post what happened, or
2. Open the service desk and create a ticket under **Security > Report an incident**, or
3. If you believe the issue is active or spreading, page the SOC duty line directly.

Include what you were doing, what happened, when you first noticed it, and any error messages or screenshots. Do not include sensitive data in the chat message beyond what is needed.

## What happens next

1. A SOC analyst triages the report and assigns an incident number.
2. You will be asked to confirm details — usually either in the ticket or in a short call with the analyst.
3. The SOC leads the response. You may be asked to help, but the SOC decides what to do and when.
4. You will get an update when the incident is closed.

## Don't investigate yourself

Do not keep clicking around to "see what happened". Do not mention the incident in the channel of the affected system before the SOC asks you to. Do not try to fix or undo what happened yourself. Stop what you were doing and report it.

## If it goes wrong

If you already reported something and then realise you know more, add a comment to the ticket or reply in the thread. If you reported something by mistake, say so in the same place — that is fine and happens regularly. If you are unsure whether something new is part of the same incident, report it as a new item and let the SOC connect the two.
