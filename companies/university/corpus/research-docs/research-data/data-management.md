# Research data management

**Owner:** Research Governance & Integrity  
**Last edited by:** Rania Gashi (Research Ethics and Integrity Officer)

## Data management plans at bid stage

All bids going through Research & Innovation Services must include a data management plan (DMP) as part of the costing pack. The DMP is reviewed by Research Governance & Integrity before the bid goes to the faculty approver. Paul's team won't take a bid to cost if the DMP section is blank.

The DMP should cover:

- What data the project will generate, and in what formats
- Where data will live during the project (normally the research data store)
- Whether any existing data will be reused, and under what licence
- Any restrictions on sharing (funder requirements, third-party data, personal data)
- A named person responsible for day-to-day data management — usually the RA or PhD student, not the PI

Rania reviews DMPs for consistency with the funder guidance and our own storage limits. If the project needs more than the standard research data store allocation, the DMP must say so and the request goes to Research Governance & Integrity for sign-off.

The costing tool has a DMP template attached to the project record. If you can't find it, ask in #research-office.

## Research data store

The research data store is the supported location for active research data. It is backed up nightly and is on the network drives for Hollins Hill and City campus; KL campus researchers access it through the VPN.

Standard quota:

- 200 GB per project by default
- Up to 1 TB with approval from Research Governance & Integrity
- Anything above 1 TB needs a written request via the IT service desk portal, and we'll ask what the data is and whether it can live elsewhere

The store is not for personal files, teaching material, or departmental admin. If you need space for those, use the appropriate shared drive or contact the IT service desk.

Files in the store are visible to the PI and any named delegate. If a researcher leaves mid-project, the PI should update the access list via the IT service desk portal.

## Depositing data at project end

When a project closes, the PI is expected to deposit a final dataset in the university repository. Research & Innovation Services sends a reminder when the award is closed on the research grants system.

Deposit should include:

- The final version of the data files
- A readme file describing the files, formats, and any code needed to open them
- A metadata record with title, creators, funding information, and a licence

If the data can't be deposited — because of confidentiality, third-party rights, or the funder's terms — the PI should note this on the award record in the research grants system. Research Governance & Integrity will review these exceptions at each quarterly research committee meeting.

The repository assigns a DOI at deposit. PIs should include this in any publications arising from the project.

## Retention after project end

Data deposited in the repository is kept for a minimum of 10 years from the end of the project. This applies regardless of whether the PI is still at Aldercombe.

For projects where data is **not** deposited (exception cases above), the PI is responsible for keeping the data for the same period. If the PI leaves, the head of school is responsible for the data. The research data store allocation is kept for 12 months after project close to allow for deposit; after that the space is reclaimed unless the PI has requested an extension through the IT service desk portal.

Research data is not covered by the general records retention schedule — it is treated as research output, not administrative record. If you have a question about whether specific data falls under a retention hold, contact Research Governance & Integrity rather than the records team.
