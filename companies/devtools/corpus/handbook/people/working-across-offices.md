# Working across Lisbon and Portland

Gantline has two main offices — Lisbon and Portland — and most teams have at least a few people in each. The distance between them means an eight-hour gap in the working day. Lisbon runs ahead, so anything that needs a quick answer from Portland usually has to wait until the late Lisbon afternoon.

The overlap is short: roughly the last two hours of the Lisbon day, which is the first two hours of the Portland morning. That window is when most cross-office conversations happen, and it is worth protecting it for the things that genuinely need both sides live. Meetings that can be avoided should be, but when you do schedule one across offices, try to land it inside that window. Anything outside it means someone is starting early or finishing late, and that should be the exception.

The default for cross-office work is writing things down. Team updates, design notes, decisions, and even short status exchanges belong in the team's documentation or a shared channel rather than in a meeting. A well-written update in `#engineering` or a team channel reaches everyone in both offices at the time that suits them, and it leaves a record that people in other time zones can catch up on. If you find yourself about to book a meeting just to share information, stop and write it up instead.

All-hands and company-wide broadcasts are recorded. If you cannot attend the live session because of your hours, watch the recording afterwards. For anything time-sensitive that was announced, the relevant team will usually post a summary in chat as well; the recording is there for the detail.

Remote days are handled the same way in both offices. You can work from home on your scheduled remote day, and the expectation is that you are reachable in chat during your normal working hours. The cross-office guidance applies to remote days too: write things down, and do not expect a reply from the other office until their day starts.

If you are planning to work from another country for a few weeks, talk to your manager and to People operations first. The company supports this for short stretches, but there are tax and employment implications that depend on where you are going and how long you will be there. Ibrahim Santos and Ebony Muller in People operations handle these requests, and they will tell you what is needed before you book anything. The `handbook/people/leave-and-absence.md` page covers the leave side of this; the working-from-another-country piece is handled case by case.

The practical rules of thumb:

- Default to asynchronous. Write the update, the question, or the decision down.
- Use the overlap for live discussions, pairing, and reviews that need both sides.
- Record meetings that people in the other office cannot attend; share the recording in the channel.
- For anything that affects the whole company, assume half the company will read it later and write accordingly.
- Keep your calendar honest about your working hours, and respect the other office's hours when booking.

If you are new to working across the two offices, or you are joining a team that spans both, your manager should walk you through how that team handles the overlap. Some teams run a daily check-in in the overlap; others rely entirely on written updates. Ask what the convention is and follow it.
