# Council, Senate and committees

Aldercombe's governance divides into three layers. Council is the governing body and holds the university's overall direction and solvency; Senate is the academic authority and owns everything to do with teaching, examining and research quality; the Executive Board runs the university day to day and carries out the decisions of the other two.

## Council

Council is the university's governing body, roughly twenty members including independent members, staff and student representatives, and the Vice-Chancellor. It approves the strategic plan, the annual budget, major capital spending, and appointments to the most senior posts. It also receives the annual report and financial statements laid before it by the Audit and Risk Committee and the Finance Committee.

Council meets five times a year, normally in October, December, February, May and July. Papers for Council go through the University Secretary's office; the deadline is usually ten working days before the meeting, and items that need a decision are preferred over items that are purely for information. If a paper is not ready by the deadline, it waits for the next cycle unless the Chair agrees it is urgent.

## Senate

Senate is the academic authority. It approves new programmes and major changes to existing ones, oversees the regulations for assessment and progression, and receives reports from the faculty boards and the Education Committee and the Research Committee. It also makes recommendations to Council on the conferment of degrees and honorary awards.

Senate meets four times a year, roughly once a term plus a summer meeting to ratify exam board outcomes. The Academic Registrar's office runs the agenda. Departmental papers usually go through a faculty board first, so a paper that starts in a department will normally take two cycles to reach Senate.

## Executive Board

The Executive Board is the senior management team, chaired by the Vice-Chancellor. It owns operational delivery: the annual plan, staffing and resources, student recruitment and retention, the estates programme, and the performance of the professional services directorates. It meets most weeks in term time and fortnightly outside it.

The Executive Board does not normally take papers from outside the University Executive Board members' own portfolios. If you think something needs to reach it, the route is through the relevant director or dean.

## Committees

Audit and Risk Committee reports to Council and oversees internal and external audit, the risk register, and whistleblowing. It meets four times a year, with the internal auditors present for part of each meeting.

Finance Committee also reports to Council. It reviews the budget, the month-end position, the capital programme and treasury matters, and makes recommendations to Council on the annual budget and fees. It meets five times a year, ahead of Council.

Education Committee reports to Senate and owns the quality assurance framework, programme approval and review, and academic standards. Research Committee reports to Senate and oversees research strategy, the research centres, and the ethics review structure. Both meet termly.

## Getting a paper onto an agenda

Each committee has a secretary who owns the agenda. The first step is to contact that secretary with a short summary of the paper and what decision is being sought. The secretary will tell you which meeting is realistic and what the deadline is. Papers should set out the context, the options considered, the recommendation, and the financial or risk implications. For Senate and its committees, the paper needs to show where it has come from — a faculty board, a department, a working group — and confirm that the relevant professional service has been consulted.

Meeting papers are circulated through the document management system, not by email. If you are new to writing a paper, the committee secretary will happily look at a draft before the formal deadline.

## The annual cycle

The academic year runs from September, but the governance cycle starts earlier. The July Council meeting approves the budget for the coming financial year (which begins in August), and the October Council meeting approves the annual report and financial statements. The February Council meeting is the main one for the strategic plan, with the May meeting reserved for any mid-year adjustments.

Committee cycles are set to feed into Council: Finance and Audit and Risk meet before each Council, and the Education and Research Committees report to Senate the meeting before Senate reports to Council. The University Secretary's office publishes the full schedule of meetings and deadlines at the start of the financial year, and it stays on the governance pages of the intranet.

Questions about which committee owns a particular matter should go to Legal Services & Governance in the first instance.
