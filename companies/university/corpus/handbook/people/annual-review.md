# Annual review (PDR)

The annual performance and development review (PDR) is the university's main cycle for reviewing your work over the past year, setting objectives for the year ahead, and having a structured conversation about your development. Every member of staff on a substantive contract is expected to take part, including those on probation (where the PDR forms part of your probation review conversation).

The review conversation is normally between you and your line manager. If your line manager has changed during the year, the review should still go ahead with your current line manager, and you should both take account of any objectives or feedback from the earlier part of the year. You can ask to include someone else from your team or from People & Culture if that would help — for example, if you and your manager have not worked together for the full year.

## Setting objectives

Objectives for the coming year should be agreed during the review, not handed to you afterwards. They should be specific enough that you and your manager can tell whether they have been met, and they should cover your day-to-day role as well as any projects or changes your team is taking on. Most roles will have between three and five objectives. At least one objective should relate to your professional development — this might be a course, a qualification, or a piece of work that stretches you.

Objectives can be revised during the year if your role changes or a piece of work is delayed. You do not need to wait for the next review to do this — raise it with your manager, and if the change is significant, note it on the PDR record so that the end-of-year conversation starts from the current position.

## The academic promotion round

For academic staff, the PDR feeds into the annual promotion round. The promotion round opens in the spring term, with a closing date published by People & Culture. If you are thinking of applying, your PDR is the right place to discuss it — your manager can tell you what evidence is expected, and whether your current objectives are building towards a promotion case. Applications are considered by a panel drawn from the faculty and the University Executive Board. The outcome of a promotion application is separate from the PDR itself; a successful review does not guarantee promotion, and an unsuccessful application does not change your PDR outcome.

## Recording the review

Your manager will record the review in the HR system using the PDR form. The form asks for a summary of the past year, the agreed objectives for the coming year, any development actions, and (for academic staff) a note of any promotion discussion. You will be asked to confirm the record before it is closed. If you disagree with any part of it, you can add a note to the record rather than confirming it.

If you have not had a PDR conversation and your review window is approaching its end, speak to your manager in the first instance. If that does not resolve it, contact People & Culture and we will chase it on your behalf.

## Raising a concern about your review

If you are unhappy with the outcome of your review, or with the way the conversation was handled, you should first raise it with your manager. Most concerns are about a disagreement over objectives or a difference of view on how the year went, and these are usually resolved in conversation. If you do not feel able to raise it with your manager, or you have done so and the matter is not resolved, you can contact People & Culture (Olga or Freya can help) and ask for a facilitated conversation. The staff grievance procedure is available if the matter is more serious, and is described in the staff handbook.

The PDR is not used as a disciplinary process. It is a forward-looking conversation, and the written record should reflect that.
