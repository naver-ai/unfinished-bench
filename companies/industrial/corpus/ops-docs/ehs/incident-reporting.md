# Accidents and near misses

**Owner:** Health, safety & environment  
**Last edited by:** Malik Nakamura (HSE Advisor)  
**Applies to:** All Kessendorf sites — Reutlingen, Telford, Greenville

This document covers what to do when someone is hurt or when something happens that could have hurt someone. It also covers the reporting duties we have to the authorities at each site.

## 1. Immediate response

1. **Get help first.** If someone needs urgent medical attention, call the local emergency number (112 in Germany, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US) before doing anything else.
2. **Make the area safe.** If you can do so without putting yourself at risk, stop the machine or process that caused the incident and keep people away from the area.
3. **Tell your supervisor or shift lead** as soon as possible. For shift changes, use the handover notes in `ops-docs/plants/shift-handover.md` so the next shift knows what happened.
4. **First aid:** For anything beyond minor cuts or bruises, send the person to the site first-aid room or the nearest occupational health contact. Reutlingen: the test lab has a first aid kit at the west entrance; Telford: the drives line has two kits, one at each end of the line; Greenville: the kit is in the break room off the plant floor.

## 2. Reporting in the EHS reporting tool

Every accident, first-aid case, and near miss **must** be entered into the EHS reporting tool, even if no one was hurt. The tool is accessible from the intranet under *EHS → Report an incident*.

1. Open the EHS reporting tool and select **New report**.
2. Choose the type:
   - **Accident** — someone was injured
   - **Near miss** — no injury, but something could have caused one
   - **First aid** — treatment given on site, no further medical care needed
3. Fill in:
   - Date and time of the event (use the time-recording terminal clock if you are unsure)
   - Site, building, and exact location (e.g., "Telford, drives line, station 4")
   - What happened — describe it in plain words, step by step
   - Who was involved (name, employee number from the HR system)
   - What was being done at the time (job, machine, tool)
   - Any witnesses
   - Immediate actions taken (first aid given, machine stopped, area cordoned off)
4. Attach photos if you have them. The tool accepts images up to 10 MB.
5. **Submit the report before the end of your shift.** If you are not able to (e.g., you are accompanying someone to hospital), ask your supervisor to submit it on your behalf.

> The HSE team reviews every new report. For accidents and serious near misses, one of us will follow up within the next working day.

## 3. Investigations

The HSE team decides which incidents need a formal investigation. In general:

- **Lost-time accidents** and **any incident where someone is sent to hospital** are always investigated.
- **Near misses** are reviewed monthly by the site HSE lead; patterns are acted on at the quarterly site safety committee.

If you are asked to take part in an investigation, cooperate fully. Investigations use the 8D method (see `ops-docs/quality/nonconformance.md` for the general approach). The findings go into the EHS reporting tool as a follow-up item, and corrective actions are tracked there until they are closed.

## 4. Statutory reporting

### Germany (Reutlingen)

- Reportable accidents (more than three days off work) must be reported to the **Berufsgenossenschaft** (BG). The HSE team submits this through the BG portal, usually within three working days of knowing the person will be off.
- The accident must also be entered in the accident register. The EHS reporting tool creates the register entry automatically when you mark the report as an accident.
- If in doubt, report it — the HSE team will decide whether it is notifiable.

### United Kingdom (Telford)

- **RIDDOR** applies. The HSE team reports to the Workplace Safety Inspectorate (WSI):
  - Any accident where someone is off work for more than seven days
  - Specified injuries (fractures, amputations, etc.)
  - Dangerous occurrences (including certain near misses on the drives line)
  - Work-related diseases
- The report must reach the WSI within the legal time limit (within 15 days for over-seven-day injuries, and without delay for specified injuries). Do not wait for a full investigation before telling the HSE team.

### United States (Greenville)

- **{{SAFETY_REGULATOR_2|code}} recordkeeping** applies to the Greenville plant. Cases that involve medical treatment beyond first aid, days away from work, restricted work, or loss of consciousness are recorded on the {{SAFETY_REGULATOR_2|code}} 300 log.
- The HSE team maintains the log in the EHS reporting tool. The annual summary is posted in the plant break room each February.
- Report the incident to the HSE team as soon as possible — do not wait for the weekly report.

## 5. If it goes wrong

- **You did not report it at the time.** Submit the report as soon as you remember, and note the delay and the reason in the *Additional comments* field. Late reports are better than none.
- **You are not sure whether it counts as a near miss.** Report it. The HSE team would rather close a report than miss an event.
- **The EHS reporting tool will not accept your entry.** Take a screenshot of the error, send it to the IT service desk, and email the HSE team with the details so we can enter it manually.
- **Someone else witnessed the event and you did not.** Ask them to submit their own report referencing yours, or ask the HSE team to link the two.
- **The injured person went to external medical care without the HSE team knowing.** Tell the HSE team the same day — there are statutory deadlines that start from the moment we know.
- **You are asked to cover up or delay a report.** Do not. Say so in the EHS reporting tool and contact the HSE manager directly.
