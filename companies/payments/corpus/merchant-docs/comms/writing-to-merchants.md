# Writing to merchants

**Owner:** Incident & merchant communications  
**Last edited by:** Zeynep Bergstrom (Communications Specialist)  
**Applies to:** Customer Communications, Merchant Success, Support, Compliance, Risk Controls, and anyone drafting outbound merchant-facing text.

---

## Tone of voice

- Write as Kilbarra, not as an individual. Use "we" and "our".
- Be direct and factual. Say what happened, what we are doing about it, and what the merchant needs to do, if anything.
- Acknowledge impact without over-apologising. One clear apology is better than several vague ones.
- Do not use jargon, internal acronyms, or system names (the back office, internal tool names, etc.) in merchant-facing text.
- Never blame a third party, a vendor, or a specific Kilbarra team in writing. If something went wrong, own it.

## Plain English

- Target a reading age similar to a broadsheet newspaper. Short sentences, active voice.
- Lead with the key message. Put background after the action or impact.
- Use the merchant's own terms where possible: "payout", "settlement", "payment link", "merchant account", not "the platform", "the system", "the ledger".
- Spell out dates and times in full. Use the merchant's local timezone and include the timezone name.
- Do not use "shortly", "soon", "in due course". Give a specific window (for example, "within two working days") or say when the next update will be sent.
- If a fee or charge is mentioned, name the exact amount and the billing line it will appear on (see invoices-and-fees.md in merchant-docs/billing).

## What never goes in an email or notice

- Full card numbers, PANs, or any card data. Refer to "the card on file" or the last four digits only.
- Passwords, one-time codes, API keys, or secrets of any kind. Do not ask the merchant to send these to us either.
- Internal URLs, staff names, or ticket IDs. Use the merchant dashboard or the public helpdesk contact instead.
- Anything that looks like a link but is not a real Kilbarra URL. Always check the domain before sending.
- The word "fraud" in a headline or subject line.
- References to our regulators or the sponsor bank.

## Translations

- For notices sent to merchants in the EU, provide versions in English plus the relevant official language(s) where we have a merchant base: Irish English is fine as the English version; also produce German, French, Spanish, Dutch, Polish, and Lithuanian where the merchant's correspondence language is one of those.
- Use an approved translation provider via the Customer Communications team. Do not send machine-translated text without a human review.
- Keep the English version as the master. If there is a discrepancy, the English version governs.
- For one-off operational notices, a translation may be sent within one working day of the English version, but the English version should not wait.

## Accessibility basics

- Use plain HTML or the template in the Customer Communications tool. Do not send attachments when a body text version is possible.
- Write descriptive link text — "view your payout report in the dashboard", not "click here".
- Use a font size and contrast that works on a phone. Most merchants read on mobile.
- Provide a plain-text version alongside any HTML email and make sure the text version is complete, not a teaser.
- If the notice includes a table, keep it simple and repeat the header row. Complex tables should be summarised in prose first.
- Mention how to get help: the helpdesk contact for their region, and the merchant dashboard self-serve options.

## Review and sending

- Draft in #customer-comms before sending anything beyond the standard templates.
- For scheduled notices (fees, plan changes, feature updates), use custnotify and follow the template library.
- Incident-related notices are drafted and sent by the Incident & merchant communications team; the procedure is in `merchant-docs/comms/customer-notices.md`.
- Keep a copy of what was sent, to whom, and when in the Customer Communications folder. The records retention schedule applies.
