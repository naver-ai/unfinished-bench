# Performance conversations

Performance conversations at Brakwater happen twice a year, once in the first half and once in the second half of the financial year. The first cycle runs from February through April and the second from September through November, with each office running its own scheduling but following the same structure. Managers are expected to hold a dedicated conversation with each direct report, separate from regular 1:1s, and to book the time well in advance through the HR system so that people can prepare.

We stopped using ratings two cycles ago. The previous five-point scale created more noise than signal, especially when we compared people across teams with very different work rhythms — an imagery ops engineer and a data scientist working on the foundation model were never going to map cleanly onto the same labels. Instead, every conversation is structured around three questions: what has gone well since the last check-in, what has been harder than expected, and what should change in the coming six months. The output of each conversation is a short written summary that the manager saves in the HR system and shares with the employee. There is no score attached and no ranking between colleagues.

The calibration step happens after each cycle. For the first cycle this is led by Femke's team, with Sam covering the Denver office and Carlos covering Rotterdam. Each team lead walks through their summaries with the People partner for their site, looking for patterns: where people are consistently stretched too thin, where the same kind of support keeps coming up, and where there might be a gap between what a role promised and what the work actually involves. Calibration is not about adjusting anyone's output — it is about making sure the People team and the executive team understand how work is actually landing across the company. After each calibration round, a short anonymised note is shared in #announcements so everyone can see the themes that came up.

If you have a concern about how a performance conversation went — for example if you felt the summary did not reflect what you discussed, or if your manager was not prepared, or if something in the process felt unfair — you can raise it with your local People partner directly. For Rotterdam that is Carlos, for Denver it is Sam. You can also reach out to Femke if you would rather speak to the Head of People. Concerns raised this way are taken seriously and are not shared with your manager unless you ask us to. If the concern is about your manager specifically, please go straight to the People team rather than trying to resolve it in the conversation itself.

A few practical notes that tend to come up during each cycle. First, the summary is meant to be a working document, not a record for some future process — it should be useful to you and your manager over the following months, so write it in language you both recognise. Second, if you joined within the last three months before a cycle starts, your check-in is a shorter conversation focused on how the onboarding is going rather than the full structure. Third, managers should make sure the conversation happens before the end of the cycle window, not on the last day of the window — if you have not heard from your manager a couple of weeks into the cycle, nudge them through chat or ask your People partner to help schedule it.

The old performance system used a separate tool called perfctl for storing summaries. We have moved fully to the HR system now, and any historical records from perfctl have been imported there. If you ever need to look back at a previous summary, it will be in your HR record under the performance section.

For anything not covered here, the People team is the first stop — either through #ask-people or by booking time with your local partner.
