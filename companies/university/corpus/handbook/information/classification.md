# Information classification

**Owner:** Information Security  
**Last edited by:** Bilal Ahmadi, Information Security Analyst

This note sets out the four information classes we use at Aldercombe, where each class may be stored, and how documents in each class should be labelled. If you are unsure which class applies to a document or dataset, start with the most restrictive class that could reasonably apply and ask Information Security if you need confirmation.

## The four classes

| Class | What it covers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Information intended for anyone, inside or outside the university. No access restrictions. | Course pages on the website, prospectus material, press releases, the academic year calendar, publicly listed committee membership. |
| **Internal** | Information for Aldercombe staff and, where relevant, our subsidiary and partner staff. Not for public distribution, but no particular harm if it were shared within the university community. | Committee papers, most finance and HR policies, room booking schedules, teaching timetables, internal meeting notes, most estates and IT service documentation. |
| **Confidential** | Information that is restricted to named individuals or teams. Unauthorised disclosure would cause real harm to individuals or to the university. | Student records (including module marks and personal details), most research data before publication, supplier contracts, individual pay and pension records, student casework, complaints and support plans. |
| **Highly confidential** | Information whose disclosure would cause serious harm, or that is subject to specific legal or regulatory duties beyond ordinary data protection. | Health and disability information, safeguarding records, certain research data subject to a data sharing agreement with explicit handling terms, pre-publication intellectual property where a funder requires restricted access, referral letters and medical evidence in student casework. |

|---|---|---|

Documents shared in chat channels such as #general, #finance or #estates are internal unless the content is confidential. |
| **Confidential** | University {{OFFICE_SUITE}} with access restricted to the relevant team, the student record system, the research grants system, the supporter CRM, the student casework system, the lettings system. | Do not store in shared locations accessible to all staff. Do not send by email to a distribution list. |
| **Highly confidential** | University {{OFFICE_SUITE}} with access restricted to named individuals, the student casework system, the Student Services file store where the folder is access-controlled, the research grants system where the award terms require restricted handling. | Store in a folder whose permissions are set to named people only, not a team. If a document contains both health information and other confidential material, treat the whole document as highly confidential. |

## Labelling

For documents that are shared outside their originating team — attachments to emails, documents placed in a shared folder, papers for a committee — add a label at the top of the document, in the header or on the cover page, in this form:

- **Public** — no label needed, but if you add one use "Public".
- **Internal** — "Internal — Aldercombe staff".
- **Confidential** — "Confidential — [team or named role] only".
- **Highly confidential** — "Highly confidential — named individuals only" and, where practical, list the individuals.

For email, add the same label to the subject line as a prefix, for example "Confidential — Student casework: [subject]". If you are forwarding a labelled document, keep the label.

For printed material, write the label on the cover or first page. Do not leave confidential or highly confidential printouts on shared printers. The estates team can advise on shredding bins for confidential paper.

## Handling notes

- Most business systems (the student record system, the finance system, the HR and payroll system, the supporter CRM, the student casework system and the rest) have their own access controls. Being able to sign in to a system does not mean every record in it is internal; records within these systems carry their own class.
- Research data often has conditions set by the funder or by a data sharing agreement. Where those conditions say something stricter than this table, follow the conditions. Tariq Lambert's team (Research Development) can point you to the relevant agreement.
- The Malaysia campus and Kestrel Vale Academy operate under the same classification scheme; tenancy records and pupil and family contact details are confidential.
- If you are copying Information Security on a document because it might be confidential or highly confidential, say which class you think it is and why. We would rather you over-classify than under-classify.
- For Freedom of Information requests, the FOI team decides what can be released; a document marked "Internal" is not automatically exempt. See the FOI note in the handbook for how that process works.
