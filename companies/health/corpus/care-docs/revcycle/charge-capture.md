# Charge capture and late charges

**Owner:** Revenue Cycle (hospital and clinic billing)  
**Last edited by:** Ethan Gupta  
**Applies to:** All clinical departments and clinics

Charge capture is the responsibility of the department that provides the service. The revenue cycle team reconciles charges to the schedule and follows up on missing or incomplete charges. This page covers charge lag targets, the late charge report, reconciliation steps, and the common misses we see.

## Charge lag targets

Charge lag is measured in working days from the date of service to the date the charge appears in the billing system. Targets are set by department and are reviewed each quarter by the Revenue Cycle Lead.

| Department | Target (working days) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency Department (all campuses) | 2 | Charges must be closed before the next shift handoff. |
| Inpatient nursing units | 3 | Pharmacy and supply charges often trail the room charge. |
| Outpatient clinics (primary care and specialty) | 3 | Same-day visit charges expected. |
| Urgent care | 1 | Same-day close required. |
| Infusion centre (South Bend) | 2 | Infusion time must be verified against the medication record. |
| Imaging and radiology | 3 | Technical and professional components are separate. |
| Cath lab and interventional radiology | 4 | Implantable device charges need the device log. |
| Surgical services (all campuses) | 5 | Includes implants, disposables, and recovery room time. |
| Cork medical centre | 3 | Local GP visit charges; employer-group billing is handled separately. |

Anything past these targets shows up on the late charge report.

## Late charge report

The late charge report runs twice a week from the scheduling and registration system and the EHR. It lists encounters with no charge, or with a charge below the expected minimum for the department. The report is delivered to the revenue cycle team mailbox and is also visible in the revenue cycle dashboard.

- The report is sorted by department, then by charge lag in days.
- Encounters past target appear at the top.
- The report includes the encounter number, patient name, date of service, department, and the expected charge minimum if one exists.

The revenue cycle analyst assigned to the department follows up with the department's charge entry staff. For lag beyond 10 working days, the Revenue Cycle Lead sends a notice to the department manager.

## Reconciling charges to the schedule

At the end of each charge cycle, the revenue cycle team reconciles the charges entered against the scheduled encounters in the scheduling and registration system. The steps are:

1. Pull the schedule for the date of service range.
2. Match each scheduled encounter to a charge in the billing system.
3. Flag any encounter with no charge or with a charge that does not match the expected department minimum.
4. For flagged encounters, check whether the encounter was cancelled, no-showed, or left without being seen. If so, confirm the status is correct in the scheduling system.
5. Send the list of unmatched encounters to the department charge entry staff with a response due date.

The reconciliation is done by the Patient Account Representatives on the revenue cycle team. Each representative is assigned a set of departments.

## Common misses

The most frequent issues found during reconciliation:

- **Supplies** — Supply charges are sometimes entered as a single lump sum instead of itemised. If a supply has a charge code, it must be entered separately. This is most common in the ED and on the inpatient units.
- **Infusion times** — Infusion charges are based on the start and stop times documented in the medication record. The documented times sometimes disagree with the charge entry. The charge entry staff should use the times in the medication record, not the times in the appointment schedule.
- **Observation hours** — Observation charges are based on the number of hours. If the observation end time is not closed out in the EHR, the charge defaults to the minimum and has to be corrected.
- **Minor procedures** — Procedures done in the exam room (sutures, splints, skin biopsies) are sometimes missed when the visit charge is entered. The procedure has its own charge code.
- **Injections and vaccines** — These are separate from the visit charge. The administration fee and the product are two lines.
