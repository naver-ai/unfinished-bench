# Research ethics review

**Owner:** Research Governance & Integrity  
**Last edited by:** Rania Gashi (Research Ethics and Integrity Officer)

## Levels of review

Ethics review runs at three levels, depending on the project:

1. **School-level review** – for routine projects with no significant ethical risks, where the school has a designated reviewer. Your school research lead can tell you who that is.
2. **Faculty-level review** – for projects that need more than one reviewer, or where the school reviewer is also a named researcher on the project. The Faculty Research Ethics Committee (FREC) handles these.
3. **University Research Ethics Committee (UREC)** – for projects involving {{PUBLIC_HEALTH_SERVICE_1|code}} patients or staff, prisoners, adults lacking capacity, or research that raises novel or sensitive issues. UREC also sees any project where the FREC asks for a second opinion.

If you are unsure which level applies, email the Research Governance & Integrity team with your project title and a one-paragraph summary and we will point you to the right route.

## The ethics system

All applications go through the ethics system in the research grants system, under the "Ethics" tab. Do not email applications to reviewers.

To submit:

1. Open the research grants system and go to **Ethics > New application**.
2. Select the school from the dropdown. If the project is cross-faculty, pick the school of the lead applicant.
3. Complete the form. The system saves as you go, so you can come back to it.
4. Attach your participant information sheet, consent form, and any data collection instruments. If you are adapting templates from your school, check they carry the current version number.
5. Submit. The system routes it to the school reviewer automatically.

## Turnaround

- School-level: aim for **5 working days** from submission to a decision.
- Faculty-level: **10 working days**.
- UREC: **15 working days** from the committee meeting date. UREC meets once a month during term time; the meeting dates are in the research office calendar.

If the reviewer asks for changes, the clock resets when you resubmit. If you have not heard back within the expected window, chase the reviewer directly in the first instance, then the Research Governance team if that does not work.

## Research involving children or schools

Projects involving children under 18, or any research taking place in a school (including Kestrel Vale Academy), must go to **faculty-level review as a minimum**, regardless of how low-risk the project seems.

For research in schools:

- You need written consent from the headteacher (or the named contact in the academy office) before you can approach families.
- For Kestrel Vale Academy, contact the academy office through the estates helpdesk if you need a room or space on site; they are used to research visits and will point you to the right person.
- If your project involves pupils of the academy, you must also hold a current Disclosure and Barring Service check at the appropriate level. The academy office will ask to see it.

## Amendments

Approval covers the project as submitted. Any change to the methodology, participant group, or data collection instruments needs an amendment.

To request one:

1. In the ethics system, open your approved application.
2. Go to **Amendments > New amendment**.
3. Describe the change and explain why it is needed. Attach any revised documents, with tracked changes where practical.
4. Amendments go back to the same reviewer or committee that saw the original application. Minor changes (wording tweaks to a consent form, adding a second interview date) are usually approved within **3 working days**. Changes to the participant group or to what participants are told go to the full committee at its next meeting.

Do not start the amended activity until the amendment is approved.

## If it goes wrong

If a participant raises a concern about your project, or something happens during the research that might affect their safety or wellbeing, stop the activity and contact the Research Governance & Integrity team the same working day. Daniel Martins or Rania Gashi will talk you through what needs to happen. Do not try to resolve it on your own.

If the concern is about you, or about someone else on the research team, tell the team directly rather than going through your school reviewer.
