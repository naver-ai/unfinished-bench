# Release of information

**Owner:** Health information management (medical records)  
**Last edited by:** Rebecca Hansen (Clinical Documentation Specialist)

## Purpose

This document covers how we handle patient requests for copies of their health records, including valid authorizations, turnaround targets, fees, and how records are sent. It also covers court orders and subpoenas — those go to Legal, not through the normal release process.

## Who does this

The HIM release of information team handles all requests for records, whether they come from patients, their authorized representatives, or other providers. The release of information vendor handles the high-volume request processing for us; we manage the queue and do the quality checks.

## Patient requests

1. A patient can request records through the patient portal, in person at any registration desk, by phone through the HIM line, or by mail. Portal requests come into the HIM work queue automatically.
2. Verify identity before releasing anything. In person, ask for a photo ID. On the phone, verify at least three identifiers: full name, date of birth, and either the patient's address on file or the last four of their Social Security number (only if we have one on file — do not ask for the full number).
3. Check whether the patient has a signed release of information authorization on file from the last 12 months. If they do, and the request matches what the authorization covers, you can process the request directly.
4. If there is no authorization on file, send the patient the authorization form through the portal or by mail. Do not release anything until a valid authorization is returned.

## Valid authorizations

An authorization is valid when it includes:

- Patient name and date of birth
- What records are being requested (or clear language that it is the complete record)
- Who is authorized to receive the records (the patient, a family member, an attorney, another provider, etc.)
- The patient's signature (or the signature of the patient's legal representative) and the date it was signed
- An expiration date or event (for example, "expires one year from the date signed")

An authorization is **not** valid when:

- It is unsigned or undated
- It has been altered after signing (white-out, cross-outs that change the scope)
- It is more than 12 months old and the patient has not renewed it
- It is for a different purpose than the request (for example, an authorization for a research study does not cover a disability claim)

If you are unsure whether an authorization is valid, ask the HIM manager (Georgia Marchetti) before processing.

## Turnaround targets

| Request type | Target |
|---|---|
| Patient portal requests | Same working day |
| In-person requests | Same working day |
| Phone requests | Same working day |
| Mail requests | Within 2 working days of receipt |
| Authorizations returned by mail | Within 2 working days of receipt |
| Third-party requests (attorneys, insurers, other providers) | Within 5 working days |
| Large record pulls (over 500 pages or full chart from chartarc) | Within 7 working days, notify requester if it will take longer |

If we cannot meet the target, send the requester a status update explaining when they can expect the records.

## Fees

- Patients requesting their own records: no charge for the first copy, including records sent through the patient portal. Paper copies beyond the first are charged at the state rate per page plus postage.
- Third-party requests (attorneys, insurers, disability carriers): charged per page plus a search fee. The fee schedule is on the HIM shared drive under `ReleaseOfInformation/FeeSchedule`.
- Requests from other providers for continuity of care: no charge.

Fees are collected through the HIM office. We do not process payments at the HIM desk — direct the requester to the cashier at the South Bend campus, or we can invoice through the revenue cycle team.

## Sending records

1. Determine whether the patient has a portal account. If they do, send the records through the patient portal. This is the preferred method for patient requests.
2. For portal delivery, attach the records as a PDF to the patient's secure message. Do not send records to a non-portal email address.
3. For paper delivery, print the records, place them in a sealed envelope marked "Confidential Health Information," and mail them through the records office mailroom. Use certified mail for third-party requests over 100 pages.
4. For provider-to-provider requests, you may send records through the interface engine if the receiving provider is on the direct secure messaging list. Otherwise, use the portal, fax cover sheet with the standard confidentiality notice, or mail.
5. Log every release in the release of information tracking spreadsheet on the HIM shared drive. Include the date received, requester, method of delivery, and the date sent.

## The release of information vendor

The vendor handles the high-volume third-party requests (attorney letters, insurer requests, disability determinations). Their portal is where we assign requests and review their output.

1. Requests that come in through the vendor's portal are assigned to the HIM release team each working day.
2. Review the vendor's authorization check before approving a release. The vendor will flag anything they think is incomplete — if you disagree, note it in the portal and send it back.
3. The vendor produces the record pull and sends it to the requester after we approve. If the vendor's pull misses records that are clearly in the request scope (for example, they pulled clinic notes but not the radiology report from the same visit), reject the pull and note what is missing.
4. The vendor does not handle patient requests — those stay in-house.

## Court orders and subpoenas

Any court order, subpoena, or other legal demand for records goes to Legal immediately. Do not process these through the normal release workflow and do not discuss them with the requester. Send the document to Ivan Keller in Legal through the records office, and note in the tracking spreadsheet that it was forwarded.

## If it goes wrong

- **Records sent to the wrong patient.** Stop what you are doing. Notify Georgia Marchetti immediately. She will coordinate with the privacy office and the patient experience team. Do not send a second message to the patient asking them to delete it — that is handled by the privacy office.
- **Authorization is not valid but the requester is pressuring you.** Refer them to the HIM manager. Do not release anything on a verbal authorization.
- **Patient says they never requested records but a request went out.** Notify Georgia Marchetti and the privacy office. The tracking spreadsheet will show who processed the request and how identity was verified.
- **The vendor sends a pull that is clearly wrong (wrong patient, wrong date range).** Reject the pull in the vendor portal and call the vendor's account manager. Do not let the vendor release a pull you have not reviewed.
- **A patient portal message fails to send.** Try again once. If it fails again, use paper delivery and note in the tracking spreadsheet that portal delivery failed.
- **A court order arrives after hours.** Leave it in the records office drop box and send a secure chat message to the HIM channel. Legal reviews it the next working day.
