# Customer stories

Case studies and references for Kessendorf motion control, drives, torque transducers and laser encoders. Owner: Marketing & product management. Last edited by Paloma Suzuki.

## Finding a story

1. Start with a lead from Sales. The Sales Director (EMEA), Key Account Director or regional sales teams flag accounts where a Kessendorf product replaced a competitor or solved a long-running issue. Ask in #customer-orders if you need contact details for the account team.
2. Check the RMA portal for any open returns on the customer's account before you approach them — we do not feature a customer who has an unresolved issue.
3. The product manager for the relevant series (TX, MC, DU) reviews the technical claims in the draft. Sonja covers TX; Paloma covers MC; Thabo covers DU. If the story spans more than one series, pick the PM whose series is the primary subject.

## Interview

1. Send the customer a short brief: what we want to cover, how long the interview takes (30-40 minutes), and who will be present. Usually two people: one writer from Marketing & product management and one applications engineer as needed.
2. Do the interview by video call unless the customer is local to Reutlingen, Telford or Greenville — then offer to visit. Record the call with permission.
3. Ask for specifics: cycle times before and after, setup effort, scrap or rework rates, maintenance intervals. We need numbers the customer can stand behind in print.
4. Take photographs or request them from the customer. For machine builders, photos of the machine with the Kessendorf unit visible work best. For test-rig builders, a photo of the rig is fine.
5. Transcript goes into the comms-docs/customer-stories folder as a source file, named `source-<customer>-<month>.md`.

## Approval

1. Draft the story in two formats: a 500-700 word written case study and a 3-4 sentence summary for the website.
2. Send both to the customer contact with a request for written approval by email. Include the logo usage note: we use their logo on the website and in the PDF version; if they have brand guidelines, ask them to attach them.
3. The customer gets two working days to review. If they ask for changes, make them and resend. If they do not respond, follow up once — if still nothing after another two working days, park the story and tell the sales contact.
4. Once the customer approves the text and logo use in writing, save the approval email in the comms-docs/customer-stories folder as `approval-<customer>.pdf` (export from the mail client).
5. Send the approved draft to Hana Costa for sign-off before anything is published.

## Formats and publication

1. PDF case study: one page, A4, company template from comms-docs/brand/brand-basics.md. Used by sales in proposals and at trade fairs (see comms-docs/events/trade-fairs.md).
2. Website summary: the 3-4 sentence version plus a photo, published through the website update process in comms-docs/web/website-updates.md.
3. Press release: only if the customer agrees to be named publicly. The press office handles it (see comms-docs/press-desk/handbook.md).
4. The PDF and website summary are published at the same time. The sales team gets a link to the PDF in #customer-orders.

## If it goes wrong

- Customer withdraws approval after publication: remove the website summary immediately and stop distributing the PDF. Tell Hana and the sales contact. Do not argue with the customer.
- Customer disputes a number: check the transcript and the source file. If the number came from them, offer to correct it in the next revision of the PDF. The website summary gets corrected within one working day.
- Logo appears wrong (colour, resolution): fix from the customer's original files — do not take logos from the internet. If the customer sent a low-resolution file, ask for a vector version.
- We publish something the customer did not approve: remove the page and the PDF, send a personal apology from Hana to the customer contact, and review how the approval email was missed.
