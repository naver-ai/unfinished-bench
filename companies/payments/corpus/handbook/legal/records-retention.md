# Records retention

**Owner:** Legal  
**Last edited by:** Mikael Gashi, Legal Counsel

**Purpose.** This policy sets out how long Kilbarra keeps the records it creates or receives in the course of its business, where those records are kept, and how scheduled deletion is carried out. It exists so that we meet our regulatory obligations as an e-money institution, satisfy contractual and evidentiary needs, and do not keep personal data for longer than necessary.

**Scope.** This policy applies to all records held by Kilbarra, in any format, across all offices and business lines. It covers records held in our internal systems, in office filing, and with third-party processors we use.

## 1. Retention periods by record type

### 1.1 Transaction records
- **Retention period:** 6 years from the end of the financial year in which the transaction was completed.
- **Where kept:** The core ledger, the payments bus message store, and the analytics warehouse. Transaction records that are part of a settlement or payout are also held in the treasury workstation and the reconciliation tool.
- **Notes:** This period reflects the limitation period for claims under Irish law and the Monetary and Payments Supervisory Authority's expectations for e-money institutions. Records include authorisation messages, clearing and settlement records, refunds, chargebacks, and payout instructions.

### 1.2 Merchant due-diligence files
- **Retention period:** 6 years from the end of the business relationship with the merchant.
- **Where kept:** The KYB onboarding tool and, for ongoing monitoring, the transaction monitoring system. Files relating to financial crime cases are held in the case tool.
- **Notes:** When a merchant relationship ends, the file is closed out and the retention clock starts.

### 1.3 Complaints
- **Retention period:** 6 years from the date the complaint was closed.
- **Where kept:** The helpdesk, with Compliance's case notes for complaints referred to the ombudsman.
- **Notes:** This includes complaints received by phone, email, post, or through the merchant dashboard. Records of how the complaint was investigated and resolved are kept with the complaint record.

### 1.4 Finance and tax records
- **Retention period:** 7 years from the end of the financial year to which they relate.
- **Where kept:** The finance shared drive, the expenses tool, and the analytics warehouse. Supplier invoices and payment records are in the accounts payable workflow.
- **Notes:** This covers invoices, credit notes, supplier payment records, payroll records, VAT returns, and corporate tax records. It also covers safeguarding records held under the safeguarding policy.

### 1.5 HR files
- **Retention period:** 6 years from the end of employment.
- **Where kept:** The HR system.
- **Notes:** This covers personnel files, leave records, performance reviews, and disciplinary records. Payroll records are kept for 7 years under 1.4.

### 1.6 Call recordings
- **Retention period:** 90 days from the date of the call.
- **Where kept:** The call recording system used by the helpdesk and the treasury operations desk.
- **Notes:** Call recordings are kept for 90 days for quality and dispute-resolution purposes. A recording attached to a complaint in the helpdesk is part of the complaint record under 1.3.

## 2. Scheduled deletion

1.  Deletion runs are executed automatically by the systems that hold the records. The owning teams are responsible for configuring and monitoring these runs.
2.  The payments bus message store, the analytics warehouse, and the case tool each run their own deletion cycle. Deletion is logged, and the logs are kept for audit purposes.
3.  Any record that falls outside the categories above but is retained for business purposes should be reviewed by the owning team each quarter and deleted when it is no longer needed.

## 3. Changes to retention periods

1.  Where a contract or a regulator sets a different period for a type of record, Legal records it in the retention register and updates Section 1 at the next review.
2.  Legal reviews the register each quarter.
