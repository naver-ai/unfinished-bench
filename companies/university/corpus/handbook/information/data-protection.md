# Data protection basics

**Owner:** Information Governance  
**Last edited by:** Stella Jensen (Information Governance Officer)

## Purpose and scope

This document sets out the basics of data protection for all Aldercombe staff, including staff at Aldercombe Estates Services Ltd, Aldercombe University Malaysia, and Kestrel Vale Academy. It covers what counts as personal data in our context, how to recognise a subject access request, what to do if you think there has been a personal data breach, and the training you must complete. If you work with information that is more sensitive than everyday records (for example, health information held by Student Services or Wellbeing & Disability Support), the same rules apply and you should also follow any team-specific guidance you have been given.

## 1. Personal data at Aldercombe

1.1 Personal data is any information relating to an identifiable living person. At Aldercombe this most often means students (including applicants and former students), staff, research participants, alumni and donors, school pupils and their families at Kestrel Vale Academy, and tenants of our residential lettings.

1.2 It includes names, contact details, student or staff IDs, and also less obvious things: a recording of a lecture, a photo from an event, a vehicle registration captured by a car park camera, or a comment in a case note that identifies someone.

1.3 Special category data (health, ethnicity, religion, political opinions, trade union membership, biometrics, and similar) needs extra care. We hold this for students through Disability Support Plans and Student Services casework, for staff through People & Culture, and for research participants where the research ethics review has approved it. Treat it as confidential and only access it when your role requires it.

1.4 Pseudonymised data (for example, research data where names have been replaced with participant codes) is still personal data if the key that links codes to individuals exists anywhere in the university.

## 2. Subject access requests

2.1 A subject access request (SAR) is any request from an individual for a copy of their own personal data, or for information about how we use it. The person does not need to use any particular wording or mention the UK GDPR.

2.2 If you receive a request that could be a SAR — from a student, staff member, alumni, research participant, tenant, or anyone else — do not try to answer it yourself. Pass it to Information Governance (ig@aldercombe.ac.uk) within one working day. If the request arrives via the IT service desk portal, the estates helpdesk, or any other system, forward the details to the same address.

2.3 If you are unsure whether something is a SAR, treat it as one and pass it over. Information Governance will confirm with the requester what they are asking for.

2.4 Information Governance may come back to you for copies of records held in your area (the student record system, the supporter CRM, the academy's school information system, shared drives or mailboxes). Reply in the Information Governance mailbox so the response stays in one place.

## 3. Personal data breaches

3.1 A personal data breach is any incident where personal data is lost, accessed, altered, or disclosed without authorisation. Examples include sending an email to the wrong recipient, losing a USB stick containing student data, a phishing email that gives someone access to a mailbox, or a document left on a printer in a shared office.

3.2 If you become aware of a possible breach, report it to Information Governance as soon as you can. Use the breach report form on the Information Governance pages, or email ig@aldercombe.ac.uk if the form is not available. Include what happened, when, what data was involved, and who you think may have been affected. You do not need to have confirmed the facts before reporting — a suspected breach is enough.

3.3 Do not contact the affected individuals yourself. Information Governance will assess the risk and, where needed, notify the {{DATA_PROTECTION_REGULATOR}} and the individuals affected. Where the breach involves the IT service desk or estates, the team manager should also raise a ticket so there is a record.

3.4 If you are not sure whether something counts as a breach, report it anyway. There is no penalty for reporting something that turns out not to be a breach.

## 4. Mandatory training

4.1 All staff must complete the data protection training module in the HR and payroll system (listed as "Data Protection Essentials"). This includes new starters before they are given access to systems, and existing staff on the annual refresh cycle.

4.2 If you are a manager, check that your team members have completed the module. People & Culture can confirm who is outstanding.

4.3 Staff at Kestrel Vale Academy, Aldercombe Estates Services Ltd, and Aldercombe University Malaysia complete the same module; the academy's version includes a short additional section on the school information system.
