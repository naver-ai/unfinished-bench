# Budget and forecast cycle

Owner: Group controlling. Last edited by Daisuke Castillo.

The annual budget is built on a calendar-year basis to match both our financial year and the fiscal years of most customers in packaging and machine tools. Work starts six months before the year begins. In month M-6, Group controlling publishes the budget pack to the intranet with the planning calendar, the current exchange-rate assumptions for USD and GBP, and the cost-index assumptions for steel, copper and electronics components. Cost centre managers have until M-4 to return their cost plans, and until M-3 for the volume and price assumptions that feed the revenue plans. The consolidated budget is presented to the Executive board in M-2, with the final version loaded into the ERP by the end of M-1 so that the new year starts with a fully released budget.

Every cost centre manager plans the same baseline set: staffing by role (using the HR system headcount list as the starting point), external services, travel, tooling, maintenance, and any capital expenditure above the local threshold. For the production cost centres in Reutlingen, Telford and Greenville, the plan is expressed per machine hour or per unit of output using the rates from the previous quarter's actuals. The planning team in Group controlling provides the templates; the manager fills in volumes and prices, not rates. Where a manager expects a step change in efficiency — a new DU series line, the Telford drives line rebalancing — they attach a short note and the planning engineer validates the assumption before it is accepted.

Headcount planning runs alongside the cost plan. In M-4, HR provides the approved headcount by cost centre, split by direct and indirect staff, and including any open requisitions that are expected to be filled during the budget year. Cost centre managers mark each line as keep, change or new, and add a comment for any change. Group controlling reconciles the salary totals with the HR system's pay scales and the agreed merit budget from the Executive board. The result is a headcount file that becomes the reference for the monthly staffing report in the reporting pack.

Re-forecasts are done quarterly, in the third month of each quarter, and cover the remaining months of the year. The re-forecast replaces the budget for the rest of the year; the budget remains in the ERP for reference but the reporting pack compares actuals against the latest forecast. The process is lighter than the annual budget: cost centre managers update only the lines that have moved by more than five per cent, and the re-forecast is loaded by Group controlling without a full Executive board review unless the year-end EBITDA moves by more than three per cent against the approved budget. The Q4 re-forecast is the basis for the year-end outlook and is shared with the CFO before the December board meeting.

Variances are commented in the reporting pack at two levels. Cost centre managers comment their own lines in the MES and ERP actuals view by the fourth working day after month-end, using a standard phrase list for common causes — volume, price, mix, timing, one-off. Group controlling writes the executive summary that explains the top ten variances by absolute value, with a clear statement of whether each is expected to reverse, continue, or is a one-off. Comments must name the driver and the site or line affected, and should reference the ERP document number where a specific invoice or order caused the variance. Uncommented variances are chased by the controlling team by the sixth working day; the pack is published on the intranet by the eighth working day after month-end.

The budget and forecast calendars are maintained in the finance-docs folder, and the current year's templates and assumptions are always the ones attached to the latest announcement in the #finance-ops channel. Questions on the pack go to Shreya or Daisuke; questions on headcount go to the HR business partner for the site.
