# Renewal cadence

**Owner:** Customer success  
**Last edited by:** Owen Fairley  
**Applies to:** annual accounts, AMER and EMEA segments

This covers the standard renewal motion for annual contracts. The goal is a signed renewal or a clear expansion/exit decision by the time we hit the 60-day mark. If an account is on a custom billing cycle, follow the same steps relative to their contract end date.

## 120 days out

1. Open the account in the CRM and confirm the contract end date and current seat count against the admin console. Flag any mismatch to billing-ops in #billing-ops before anything else.
2. Run the usage report for the account (CRM > Account > Usage). Compare active seats to billed seats. If active seats are consistently over the billed count, note that for the expansion conversation.
3. Assign a primary renewing owner if not already set. For accounts above $50k ARR this is the assigned CSM; for smaller accounts it is the account owner in the CRM.
4. Add a renewal task in the CRM with the target date set to contract end minus 45 days. Set the stage to "Renewal — risk assessment".
5. Schedule the first check-in call with the customer contact. The call should cover product adoption, open support tickets, and any changes in their team structure.

## 90 days out

1. Review the renewal risk score in the CRM. If the score is below the "healthy" threshold, update the account notes with the reason and start a weekly internal check-in with the CSM lead.
2. Send the customer a short health check survey (the standard one in the CRM). Follow up with a call to discuss anything that comes back below a 4.
3. Pull the ticket history from the helpdesk for the last 90 days. If there are more than three unresolved tickets marked priority, escalate to the support manager before the next customer call.
4. Update the forecast in the CRM to one of: committed, best case, or pipeline. Use "committed" only when the customer has confirmed renewal in writing.
5. If the account has expansion potential (usage growth, new teams, or a recent acquisition), create a handoff note for sales. The note goes in the CRM under the account and tags the assigned AE. Do not run expansion conversations yourself unless the AE is on leave.

## 60 days out

1. Confirm the renewal quote with billing-ops. Check that the seat count on the quote matches the admin console and that any agreed discounts are reflected. Raise discrepancies in #billing-ops.
2. Send the renewal proposal to the customer. Attach the usage summary and the health check results. Copy the AE on any account with expansion potential.
3. Update the CRM stage to "Renewal — proposal sent" and set the expected close date.
4. Book the renewal review call with the customer. Aim for a decision before the 30-day mark if possible.
5. If the customer asks for changes to the proposal (seat count, term, payment terms), loop in deal desk via the CRM before agreeing anything. Redirect all pricing questions to deal desk.

## If the champion leaves

- Move quickly but do not panic. Update the account notes in the CRM with the departure date and the name of their replacement if known.
- Reach out to the new contact within two working days. Offer a walkthrough of the admin console and a health check. Do not assume they know how the account is configured.
- If the champion was the only engaged contact, treat the account as at risk and update the CRM risk score accordingly.
- If the departing champion had been the renewal decision maker, ask them (or their manager) who will own the renewal on their side. Update the account record with that name.
- If the replacement is not known by the 60-day mark, escalate to the CSM lead and the VP Customer Success. They will decide whether to involve the AE earlier than normal.

## If it goes wrong

- If the customer goes quiet for more than ten working days after the proposal, escalate to the CSM lead. Do not wait for the next scheduled check-in.
- If the customer asks to reduce seats significantly (more than 20 percent), update the forecast to best case and involve the AE. Seat reductions usually signal a broader change on their side.
- If the renewal slips past the contract end date, confirm with billing-ops whether the account will continue on a month-to-month basis and note that in the CRM. The customer success team does not set grace terms — that is billing-ops' call.
- If the account is lost, close it in the CRM with the loss reason and a short summary. The summary should be factual and useful for the next person who picks up the account.
